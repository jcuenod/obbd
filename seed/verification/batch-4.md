# Verification batch 4

Checked 83 entries (galilee-boat … jubilees) against the WEB text with `./obbd check-passages --per 2`. All MT versification notes in the batch were checked and are correct (e.g. 1Sam 21:10-15 = MT 21:11-16; 2Kgs 12:17-18 = MT 12:18-19; 1Kgs 5:1-12 = MT 5:15-26; 2Chr 2:3-16 = MT 2:2-15; Exod 8:15 = MT 8:11; Num 16:46-48 = MT 17:11-13; Deut 13:12-18 = MT 13:13-19; Mal 4:2 = MT 3:20; Dan 4:30 = MT 4:27). I found no off-by-one or wrong-chapter references. Cuneiform and Egyptian section numbers cited in notes (LE §§53-55, LH §§250-252, HL §§17-18, 187-200; Amenemope chs. 2, 4, 6, 7, 9, 23, 30; Josephus Ant./War references) match the standard editions as far as I can check. 12 entries changed.

## Changes

- **genealogies**: `Ezra 2:59-63` note "Priests excluded for lacking genealogical records" → "Returnees, including priests, unable to prove descent; the priests barred from the holy food". Vv. 59-60 concern lay families who could not prove Israelite descent. Only vv. 61-63 concern priests.
- **gibeon**: `Jer 41:12` note "'The great pool that is in Gibeon'" → "'The great waters that are in Gibeon', possibly the rock-cut pool". The Hebrew is *mayim rabbîm*, not "pool", so the old quotation was wrong. The identification with the excavated pool stays disputed.
- **gods-of-2-kings-17**: `Ezra 4:2, 9-10` rel event → setting, and the note now adds ", later than 2 Kgs 17". Ezra attributes the settlers to Esarhaddon and Osnappar (Ashurbanipal). These are later deportations, not the 2 Kgs 17 resettlement that the entry documents.
- **gudea-cylinders**: replaced `Exod 20:24` (concept, "Deity promises to come and bless at the sanctuary he designates; cf. Ningirsu's promised abundance once the Eninnu is built") with `Hag 2:15-19` (concept, "Crops blessed from the day the temple foundation is laid; cf. Ningirsu's promised abundance"). Exod 20:24 is an altar law with no temple-building context. Haggai's link between temple founding and agricultural plenty is the standard biblical comparandum for Cylinder A xi-xii.
- **habiru**: `1Sam 13:3, 19` → `1Sam 13:3, 7, 19`. Note "'Hebrews' as a Philistine label" → "'Hebrews' partly distinct from 'Israel' (13:7); a Philistine label (13:19)". In 13:3 it is Saul, not the Philistines, who uses "Hebrews". 13:7 (Hebrews crossing the Jordan) is the verse usually cited in the ʿapiru discussion.
- **hammurabi**: `Deut 4:5-8` polemic → polemic + `disputed: true`. Note "'What other great nation has statutes as just as this?'" → "'What great nation has statutes so righteous?'; cf. Hammurabi's boast of just laws". The verse gives no direct evidence of an anti-Hammurabi polemic. The contrast with the LH epilogue is a scholarly suggestion (e.g. Weinfeld), not a consensus.
- **herod-antipas**: `Luke 23:6-12` rel event → attests. No source outside Luke narrates the hearing before Antipas.
- **host-of-heaven**: `Deut 17:3` → `Deut 17:2-5`. The note says the worship is "a capital offence", but the death penalty is in v. 5.
- **jehoiachin**: `2Kgs 25:27-30` and `Jer 52:31-34` rel event → attests. No extrabiblical source records the release by Amel-Marduk. The ration tablets (c. 592) attest the king's maintenance in Babylon but not this event.
- **jehoiachin-ration-tablets**: `2Kgs 25:27-30` note "Jehoiachin, king of Judah, maintained at the Babylonian court with an allowance" → "Allowance at the Babylonian court after 562; the tablets show royal rations already c. 592". The old note implied that the tablets document the post-562 allowance, but they are some 30 years earlier.
- **jerusalem**: `2Kgs 25:8-17` → `2Kgs 25:8-10`. The note concerns the burning of the city and the destruction layers. Vv. 11-17 (deportation, temple bronzes) are not relevant.
- **josephus**: `Luke 2:1-2` note "Census under Quirinius in 6 CE (Ant. 18.1-2, 26)" → "Quirinius's census, which Josephus dates to 6 CE, after Herod's death (Ant. 18.1-2, 26)". The old note read as though Luke dates the census to 6 CE. Luke sets it under Herod, which is the crux of the problem.

## Flag for expert review

- **gath** `1Sam 17:4` (language?) and `2Kgs 12:17` (attests): the ʾLWT/WLT sherd and the identification of the siege trench with Hazael both depend on Maeir's interpretation. Both are widely accepted, but the second is not marked disputed.
- **gezer** `1Macc 13:43-48`: many Greek manuscripts read "Gaza" in 13:43. "Gazara" is an emendation (supported by Josephus and 13:53). WEB prints Gazara.
- **gilgamesh-epic** `Gen 3:5-7` (parallel?) and `Eccl 4:9-12` (parallel?): both are kept as disputed. The Eccl "threefold cord" parallel is cited from the Sumerian Bilgames and Huwawa and also from SB Gilgamesh, so the source attribution in the note may need checking.
- **gudea** `1Sam 13:14`: "after his own heart" is compared with Gudea's election formula. This rests on McCarter's reading ("of his own choosing"), and a specialist may consider it thin.
- **hiram-of-tyre** `2Sam 5:11`: the tag is fine, but synchronising Hiram I with David is chronologically problematic (Josephus/Menander dates). The entry text should note this.
- **hasmoneans** `John 4:20` (setting): the destruction of the Gerizim temple by Hyrcanus is relevant background, but the link to the Hasmonean entry is indirect.
- **hadad-rimmon** `1Kgs 20:23`: "gods of the hills" is Aramean theology in general, not about Hadad specifically. It is weak but kept.
- **jehu** `Hos 1:4` (event): Hosea alludes to, rather than narrates, the Jezreel bloodshed. `attests` or `setting` may fit better.
- **josephus** `Luke 2:1-2` (event): kept as event with a clarified note. Some scholars dispute that Luke's census is the same as Josephus's 6 CE census, so consider marking it disputed.
- **iron-technology** `Deut 3:11` (realia?): the "basalt sarcophagus" reading competes with the dolmen interpretation. The note says only "perhaps".
