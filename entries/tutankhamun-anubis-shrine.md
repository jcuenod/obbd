## Overview

A portable shrine from Tutankhamun's tomb consists of a gilded wooden chest on a sledge with carrying poles and a recumbent image of the jackal god Anubis. The excavators found it at the entrance to the tomb's Treasury. It is a well-preserved example of how Egyptian craftspeople combined a sacred chest with fittings for transport. The chest and poles offer a material comparison for the biblical ark of the covenant, while the divine image and the burial setting give this object a distinct function.

## Description and Provenance

Howard Carter's team recovered the shrine from tomb KV62 in the Valley of the Kings. It lay at the threshold of a chamber the excavators called the Treasury. The excavation records describe the figure, chest, sledge, and carrying poles together under Carter number 261. The current Griffith Institute catalogue lists its registration with the Grand Egyptian Museum as GEM 6.

## Contents

The upper figure represents Anubis in the form of a recumbent jackal. Anubis is associated with the preservation and protection of the dead. The chest beneath the figure has the form of a small shrine, finished with gilding. The combined object was set on a sledge with poles that allowed movement by bearers. Its position at the door gives the figure the appearance of guarding the contents of the Treasury.

Other Egyptian sacred objects were designed for transport, including shrines mounted on processional barques. The equipment varies across these examples. Their common feature is that a sacred object or image could remain protected or displayed while being moved as a unit.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Exod 25:10–15 describes a wooden [[ark-of-the-covenant|ark]] overlaid with gold, with poles passing through rings. Num 4:5–6 prescribes covering the chest and preparing it for designated bearers. The Egyptian shrine shows how a sacred chest could be equipped for transport, though its support and fittings differ from the system described in Exodus.

1 Chr 15:15 describes Levites carrying the ark with poles on their shoulders. 2 Sam 6:13 describes bearers in a procession, and Josh 3:3–6 places priests carrying the ark at the head of the people crossing the Jordan. The Egyptian evidence helps give physical scale and context to the descriptions. The shrine from Tutankhamun carries Anubis on top, while the biblical descriptions give the ark flanking [[cherubim|cherubim]] without a figure of Yahweh between them.

## Scholarly Issues

The Anubis shrine dates to the New Kingdom, whereas many scholars associate the detailed ark instructions in Exodus and Numbers with priestly writing. Dating those biblical texts and the practices they describe involves evidence beyond the Egyptian object. Their resemblance shows that ancient craftspeople could solve similar problems of transporting sacred furniture. Its historical significance is strongest as a comparison of forms and equipment.
