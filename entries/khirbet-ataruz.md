## Overview

Khirbet Ataruz is an Iron Age site in central Jordan generally identified with Ataroth, a town linked to Gad in Numbers and to an Israelite ruler in the Mesha Stele. Excavations have uncovered a substantial temple complex and a later sanctuary with a short inscription on a stone altar. These remains help establish the site’s religious importance. The evidence also places it within the competition between Israel and Moab, although dating individual building phases and assigning them to rulers remain matters of interpretation.

## Location and Identification

Khirbet Ataruz lies on the Moabite plateau east of the Dead Sea, in the region around [[dibon|Dibon]]. The name and location support the identification with Ataroth in Numbers and the [[mesha-stele|Mesha Stele]]. Ataroth and Ataroth-addar in Josh 16:2, 5, 7 and 18:13 belong to descriptions of territories west of the Jordan and refer to other places. The shared name need not imply a connection between the communities. [@kai, no. 181] [@cos2, 2.23] [@abd, 1:377, 1:510–511]

## Archaeological History

Excavations led by Chang-Ho Ji beginning in 2000 exposed a substantial Early Iron Age II temple complex, including ritual stands, figurines and altars. The excavators connect an earlier phase with Israelite rule and later cult construction with Moabite control. Pottery and architecture help distinguish phases, but the chronology and political identities of the builders cannot all be established directly from the remains.

A cylindrical stone altar from a later sanctuary was found in 2010. Specialists published two short inscriptions on it in a 2018 journal volume issued online in 2019. They identified early Moabite language and letter forms together with Egyptian hieratic numerals. One text appears to list amounts, and the interpretation of the other remains debated. The readers’ proposals are useful for exploring the sanctuary’s administration. Their uncertainty leaves the inscriptions an unsafe basis for reconstructing any specific event in Mesha’s campaign.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Num 32:3 lists Ataroth among the towns requested by Gad and Reuben. Num 32:34 says the Gadites built it. The stele states that the men of Gad had lived there long before Mesha, that the king of Israel built it, and that Mesha took it and settled other people there. It describes the population’s fate in relation to Chemosh and Moab. 2 Kgs 3:4–5 separately reports Mesha’s rebellion after Ahab’s death without listing Ataroth among its events. The stele and Numbers give independent evidence for associating the name Gad with the region. [@kai, no. 181] [@cos2, 2.23]

Mesha says he brought something called an *ʾrʾl dwdh* from Ataroth before [[chemosh|Chemosh]] at Kerioth. The phrase is disputed. The noun rendered “altar hearth” in Ezek 43:15–16 is one proposed comparison. The expression in 2 Sam 23:20 involving Ariel of Moab has also been discussed, though the Hebrew there is itself difficult. The object, its relation to the sanctuary and whether the expression could refer to a person remain uncertain. [@kai, no. 181] [@cos2, 2.23] [@abd, 1:377, 1:510–511]
