## Overview

Pekah son of Remaliah ruled the northern kingdom of Israel in the last decades before Samaria fell. He allied with Rezin of Damascus and fought Judah during the crisis known as the Syro-Ephraimite war. Assyrian inscriptions report the loss of Israelite territory and the replacement of Pekah with Hoshea under Tiglath-pileser III. The sources agree on the change of ruler and describe it from different perspectives. Pekah’s reported twenty-year reign in Kings is difficult to reconcile with Assyrian dates.

## Extrabiblical Sources

A summary inscription of [[tiglath-pileser-iii|Tiglath-pileser III]] names Pekah in its account of the land the Assyrians called the House of Omri. It claims extensive conquest and deportation and describes an upheaval in which the inhabitants overthrew Pekah. The Assyrian king says that he installed Hoshea and received tribute. The language presents the succession as a demonstration of imperial power. It does not name participants in the overthrow other than the ruler who was replaced and his successor.

Other [[tiglath-pileser-iii-inscriptions|Assyrian accounts]] give the wider context of campaigns against Israel and Aram-Damascus in the 730s BCE. They attest a substantial reduction of the northern kingdom before the conquest of its capital. The inscription supplies independent evidence for Pekah’s reign, its end, and Assyria’s claim to responsibility for the arrangements that followed.

## Biblical Portrayal

In 2 Kgs 15:25 Pekah kills Pekahiah and takes power. The mention of men from Gilead shows that he had support east of the Jordan. Second Kings 15:27–31 credits him with twenty years, names cities and territories taken by Tiglath-pileser, and attributes his killing to [[hoshea|Hoshea]] in a conspiracy. The Assyrian account stresses the population’s overthrow of Pekah and the emperor’s installation of Hoshea. Together the accounts allow for local action within an imperial crisis, though they do not establish the arrangements among those involved.

Second Kings 16:5–9 describes [[rezin|Rezin]] of Aram and Pekah attacking Jerusalem and [[ahaz|Ahaz]] appealing to Assyria. It reports that Tiglath-pileser captured Damascus and killed Rezin. In Isa 7:1–9 Isaiah urges Ahaz to trust in the face of the threat and speaks of installing a different ruler in Judah. The repeated description of Pekah as the son of Remaliah is often read as diminishing his status. Second Chronicles 28:5–8 emphasizes Judah’s losses and the taking of captives by Israel. These accounts present different aspects of the crisis and give different weight to diplomacy, threat, and judgment.

## Historical Assessment

The fall of Damascus around 732 BCE and the Assyrian record of Hoshea’s appointment place the end of Pekah’s rule in a relatively narrow period. A full twenty-year reign over all Israel would overlap with other kings whose reigns enter Assyrian or biblical chronology. One proposal counts years of rival rule in Gilead before the seizure of Samaria. Another treats the figure as a problem of scribal transmission or regnal reckoning. Pekah’s support from Gilead makes the first proposal possible, but the text gives no independent dates for such a rival reign. The problem remains open.
