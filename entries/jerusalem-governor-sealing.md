## Overview

A stamped clay object from the Western Wall Plaza in Jerusalem bears the Hebrew title “governor of the city.” The excavators recovered it near a late Iron Age house. The title is the same as the one borne by officials in the accounts of Josiah's reforms in 2 Kings and 2 Chronicles. The impression gives independent evidence for the office in Jerusalem, while the object's intended use and the identity of the official remain open.

## Description and Provenance

The clay was found during conservation of a wall beside a late Iron Age four-room house. The stamp shows two standing figures facing one another, holding what the excavator describes as a moon-like object. The Hebrew inscription lies below the figures. The impression itself preserves the title, with no surviving personal name beside it [@weksler-bdolah-western-wall, fig. 3].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

In 2 Kgs 23:8 a city gate is identified by association with Joshua, the governor of the city, in the account of Josiah's suppression of local shrines. In 2 Chr 34:8 Josiah sends Maaseiah, the governor of the city, with other officials to repair the temple. The two passages place officials with the title in different settings. The sealing confirms the title's use in Jerusalem independently of these accounts. Its short inscription supplies neither a name nor evidence for either episode. The [[court-officials|entry on court officials]] places the title alongside other designations known from inscriptions and biblical texts.

## Scholarly Issues

The object is often called a bulla, a term commonly used for a clay seal impression. Its excavator also describes it as a possible docket, a label that might have been placed inside a vessel to identify a sender. The original function is uncertain. The title on the stamp is stronger evidence for city administration than the clay support is for any particular method of sealing documents or marking goods. See [[seals-and-bullae]] for those distinctions.
