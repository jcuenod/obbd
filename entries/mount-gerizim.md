## Overview

Mount Gerizim rises over ancient Shechem and is the sacred mountain of the Samaritan community. Deuteronomy associates it with blessing, and the Samaritan Pentateuch also names it as the site of an altar. Excavations have uncovered the remains of a sacred precinct with Persian and Hellenistic phases and hundreds of dedicatory inscriptions. The site provides evidence for worship of the God of Israel outside Jerusalem and for the long development of a distinct Samaritan tradition centered on the mountain.

## Location and Identification

Gerizim stands south of [[shechem|Shechem]], opposite [[mount-ebal-altar|Mount Ebal]]. The sacred precinct occupied the mountain's summit. A second location, Tell er-Ras, lies on a lower spur and contains remains associated with a later Roman sanctuary. Distinguishing these areas and their periods of use is important for interpreting the history of the mountain.

## Archaeological History

The excavations directed by Yitzhak Magen identified two principal phases of the precinct. The excavators dated its foundation to the fifth century BCE, during Persian rule, and its enlargement to the early second century BCE. Scholars debate the form of the earliest buildings and how securely the surviving remains identify a temple in that phase. The later precinct stood above a substantial Hellenistic settlement and has left clearer architectural and inscriptional evidence.

Hundreds of inscriptions, most of them dedicatory and many fragmentary, include texts in Aramaic and Hebrew as well as Greek. Their references to people, offerings, and the sacred place supply evidence for local worship. This evidence is compatible with a Yahwistic sanctuary among the inhabitants of the region of Samaria. The use of later Samaritan identity as a label for every Persian-period worshiper can obscure the gradual nature of [[samaritan-origins|the separation of the communities]].

[[josephus|Josephus]] associates the foundation with Alexander's time (Ant. 11.302–347). The Persian-period remains favor an earlier start for the sacred precinct. Josephus also reports that John Hyrcanus destroyed the sanctuary (Ant. 13.254–256). The excavators date the destruction to around 111–110 BCE on the basis of the coins, later than the date that has often been inferred from Josephus's narrative. The Roman sanctuary at Tell er-Ras and a Byzantine church dedicated to the Theotokos on the summit belong to subsequent phases of the mountain's religious history.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Deut 11:29 and 27:12 associate Gerizim with blessing, and Josh 8:33 pictures half the assembly standing in front of it. Judg 9:7 places Jotham on Gerizim when he addresses the people of Shechem. The setting connects these texts with a mountain overlooking a town and a valley important in Israelite tradition.

At Deut 27:4 the Samaritan Pentateuch has Gerizim where the Masoretic Text has Ebal. Where the Masoretic form of Deut 12:5 says God will choose a place, the Samaritan Pentateuch refers to a choice already made, understood in Samaritan tradition as Gerizim. These differences show how the site became the focus of different readings of shared scriptures. In the Hellenistic period, 2 Macc 6:2 associates the Gerizim sanctuary with Zeus Xenios in the measures taken under [[antiochus-iv|Antiochus IV]]. In John 4:20–21 the woman’s appeal to ancestral worship on the mountain brings Gerizim and Jerusalem into direct conversation.
