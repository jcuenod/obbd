## Overview

An ephod is an item named in several accounts of worship in ancient Israel. Some biblical passages describe a linen garment worn by a priest or by David. Exodus gives instructions for a more elaborate vestment with a breastpiece, and Samuel associates an ephod with inquiries made to Yahweh. Judges also describes an object made from gold and set up at a sanctuary. These uses help explain why scholars differ over whether the word always refers to a garment and how it was connected with divination.

## Archaeological Evidence

Lexical evidence is especially useful because no excavated biblical ephod can be securely identified. Scholars compare Hebrew *ʾēpôd* with Old Assyrian *epattum*, a costly garment, and with Ugaritic *ipd*, a related word often understood as a garment. The comparison favors an association with clothing but gives no exact pattern or material for the biblical examples. Ancient Near Eastern temple service included providing garments for [[cult-images|divine statues]], which supplies a possible context for some biblical usages. [@abd] [@king-stager-life] [@anep]

## In the Bible

Exod 28:4–14 describes a high priest's ephod made with gold and colored yarns, joined at the shoulders and held by a band. The next instructions attach a breastpiece and place the [[urim-and-thummim|Urim and Thummim]] in it (Exod 28:15–30). This vestment differs in elaboration from the linen ephod worn by Samuel in 1 Sam 2:18 and by David in 2 Sam 6:14. The linen garment identifies participation in acts of worship without the description given for the high priest's vestment. [@abd] [@king-stager-life]

In 1 Sam 23:6, 9–12 and 30:7–8, the priest Abiathar brings an ephod before David's inquiries to Yahweh. These episodes establish an association with seeking divine guidance. They do not say exactly how an answer was obtained. The breastpiece described in Exodus has suggested the use of sacred lots or comparable objects, but the equipment in these narratives cannot be reconstructed with confidence.

In Judg 8:24–27, Gideon uses gold from the spoils to make an ephod and places it in Ophrah. The narrator treats the attention it receives as a danger. Micah makes both an ephod and [[teraphim|household cult objects]] in Judg 17:5. These examples suggest an object that could be set up or kept at a sanctuary. Some scholars propose that it originated as clothing for a divine image, comparing the precious coverings of images in Isa 30:22. The relationship among this use, the priestly garment and the instrument of inquiry remains debated. [@abd] [@ddd]

Exod 39:2–7 describes making the vestment and setting stones on its shoulder straps as reminders of the tribes of Israel. Hos 3:4 names the ephod in a list of institutions and objects Israel will lack for an extended period, alongside rulers, sacrifices and sacred stones. The list reflects the importance of access to cult objects in national life. [@abd]
