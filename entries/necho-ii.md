## Overview

Necho II was a pharaoh of Egypt's Twenty-Sixth Dynasty who ruled around 610–595 BCE. As Assyrian power collapsed, Egyptian forces intervened in Syria and the Levant. The Bible connects Necho's campaign with Josiah's death at Megiddo and describes Necho appointing a new king of Judah and imposing tribute. The Babylonian chronicles place Egypt on the side of Assyria's surviving forces and record the decisive Egyptian defeat at Carchemish. Together the sources show how a contest between empires affected Judah's royal succession.

## Extrabiblical Sources

The [[fall-of-nineveh-chronicle|Fall of Nineveh Chronicle]] describes Egyptians supporting Assyrian resistance after the loss of Nineveh. Egyptian troops took part in the attempt to recover Harran in 609 BCE. The [[nebuchadnezzar-chronicle|Nebuchadnezzar Chronicle]] records the Egyptian defeat at Carchemish in 605 BCE and the pursuit of survivors toward Hamath. The chronicles identify Egypt's role in these events even where their descriptions concentrate on the Babylonian king or army.

Herodotus credits Necho with work on a canal connecting the Nile system with the Red Sea and describes a victory at Magdolos followed by the capture of Cadytis (Histories 2.158–159). Similar names have led scholars to compare Megiddo or a place called Migdol with Magdolos, and Gaza and other locations with Cadytis. The geographical identifications carry different degrees of plausibility and cannot be used as straightforward confirmation of the biblical accounts.

## Biblical Portrayal

2 Kgs 23:29–30 connects Necho's march toward the Euphrates with [[josiah|Josiah's]] death at [[megiddo|Megiddo]]. The Hebrew wording about the king of Assyria can be understood in light of the Babylonian chronicle as going to his aid. The text gives little detail about the encounter with Josiah. 2 Chr 35:20–24 expands the episode into a battle in which Necho warns Josiah against intervening and archers wound him. The differences leave the immediate circumstances of Josiah's death open to debate.

2 Kgs 23:33–35 reports that Necho removed Jehoahaz, installed Eliakim under the name Jehoiakim, and required tribute. 2 Chr 36:3–4 also records the deposition and appointment. Jer 46:2 dates the Egyptian defeat at [[battle-of-carchemish|Carchemish]] to Jehoiakim's fourth year. The notice in 2 Kgs 24:7 that Egypt ceased to come out of its land reflects Babylon's subsequent dominance in the region.

## Historical Assessment

Necho's actions in Judah fit the wider context of Egyptian military and political activity in the Levant. Material from coastal sites such as [[mesad-hashavyahu-ostracon|Mesad Hashavyahu]], including Greek pottery, is sometimes discussed in relation to Saite military networks. Such evidence admits several explanations and does not identify a specific operation led by Necho. Jer 47:1 refers to a pharaoh attacking Gaza without naming him. Necho and Herodotus's account of Cadytis provide one possible historical comparison.
