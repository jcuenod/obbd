## Overview

11Q11 is a fragmentary Hebrew scroll from Qumran Cave 11. It preserves compositions used against harmful spirits and a version of Psalm 91. This arrangement gives evidence for how a biblical psalm could be read and used for protection in the Second Temple period. The scroll is distinct from the Great Psalms Scroll, 11Q5. Its collection also provides a possible context for the quotation of Psalm 91 in the Gospel temptation narratives.

## Description and Provenance

The manuscript belongs to the [[dead-sea-scrolls]] and is catalogued as 11Q11. The most relevant material survives in columns IV–VI. Damage to the scroll leaves gaps in the sequence and wording of the compositions. Editions distinguish the surviving parts of Psalm 91 in column VI from reconstructions of missing lines and from the other compositions [@djd23, 11Q11].

## Contents

The texts associate sacred speech with protection or the expulsion of hostile spirits. Some address a hostile being directly and draw on the authority of God. Psalm 91 expresses trust in divine protection and gives angels a role in guarding the person who seeks refuge. Its position among the other compositions makes an interpretation against harmful spirits plausible. The fragmentary scroll leaves the performance setting of each composition uncertain [@pajunen-exorcism].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

The manuscript is an ancient witness to Psalm 91 and to a particular reception of the psalm. Matt 4:5–7 and Luke 4:9–12 depict the devil quoting its promise of angelic protection to urge Jesus to jump from the temple. Jesus responds by rejecting the act as a test of God. The possibility that ancient hearers knew protective uses of Psalm 91 adds context for the exchange. The narratives do not specify such a ritual use or establish contact with this scroll.

## Scholarly Issues

The terms protective and exorcistic describe related but distinguishable purposes. A prayer can seek safety from an anticipated danger, while an exorcism addresses a spirit already understood to be present. The order of the surviving pieces and the force of their wording matter for assigning a purpose to each part. See [[exorcism-and-spirit-possession]] for material from other Jewish sources.
