## Overview

The Merneptah Stele is a granite monument from the Egyptian king's mortuary temple at Thebes. It dates to his fifth regnal year, around 1208 BCE, and mainly celebrates victory over Libyan forces. Its final lines name Canaan, several cities, and a group called Israel. This is the earliest generally accepted occurrence of Israel outside the Bible. The text establishes that a group with this name was known to Egyptian scribes in Canaan by that date. The location, extent, and political organization of the group remain uncertain.

## Description and Provenance

Flinders Petrie found the stone at western Thebes in 1896. The inscription was carved on the back of an earlier monument of Amenhotep III. The main text praises the victories of [[merneptah|Merneptah]], and the Canaanite names occur near the close. The monument is kept in Cairo [@ael] [@cos2].

## Contents

The inscription celebrates success against the Libyans. Its closing lines then refer to Canaan and name Ashkelon, Gezer, Yanoam, and Israel in a series of claims about defeat. A determinative is a hieroglyphic sign that classifies a word without being pronounced. The names of the cities carry a foreign-place classifier, while Israel has signs for a people. This suggests that the scribe regarded Israel as a population group. Egyptian spelling conventions alone provide limited evidence about what that group's institutions were.

The line about Israel also says that its seed is gone. The statement is part of the language of royal victory. Scholars differ over whether seed evokes grain supplies or descendants. Either interpretation fits rhetoric describing an opponent's ruin and gives little reason to read the claim as a count of survivors [@ael] [@anet].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Gen 32:28 (MT 32:29) explains Israel as Jacob's new name. The Egyptian text independently attests the name for a group in Canaan centuries before the surviving copies of the biblical narrative. It supplies a date against which proposals for the [[emergence-of-israel|emergence of Israel]] must be tested. It gives no genealogy or list of tribes and does not narrate the group's route into Canaan.

Judg 1:27–36 places Israel and Canaanite towns in the same region. Josh 10:33 and 16:10 portray [[gezer|Gezer]] as a Canaanite city, while 1 Kgs 9:16 describes a later Egyptian capture and transfer. These biblical episodes fit the broader importance of Gezer as an identifiable city under shifting powers. The stele concerns an earlier claim and supplies no confirmation of the later events.

## Scholarly Issues

Most scholars accept the reading Israel. Alternatives, including a form of Jezreel, have found little support. Proposals locating the group in the central hills, the Jezreel region, or farther east remain debated. Frank Yurco reassigned some Karnak reliefs to Merneptah and associated one group of figures with Israelites. Anson Rainey and others have read the relevant figures differently. A possible earlier occurrence of the name on a broken statue pedestal in Berlin remains disputed. These proposals rely on evidence that is less secure than the spelling on the stele.
