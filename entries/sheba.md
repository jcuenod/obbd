## Overview

Sheba is the biblical name commonly associated with Saba, an ancient kingdom centered at Marib in what is now Yemen. Its inscriptions, temples, and irrigation works attest an important South Arabian state that traded in aromatics and other goods. Kings describes a queen of Sheba visiting Solomon with gifts and questions. No known Sabaean inscription identifies her. The trade and wealth evoked by the account suit connections known from the Iron Age, while its date and the historical circumstances of the visit remain uncertain.

## Sources

Sabaean inscriptions in the South Arabian alphabet record rulers, dedications, construction, and war. Their early chronology remains debated. Assyrian records supply some absolute chronological points for contact with rulers identified as Sabaeans. Sargon II mentions Itʾamar, and inscriptions associated with Sennacherib name Karibʾil in a record of gifts. These notices show contact across long distances. They leave open the nature and regularity of many transactions involving southern Arabia.

At Marib the remains of a major dam and channels document investment in irrigation. Temples at Awwam and Barʾan are associated with Almaqah, a prominent Sabaean god. These structures give evidence for cooperation and organization in a dry environment. Their remains span different phases and cannot supply a single date for the rise of Saba or its commercial network.

## History

Saba benefited from routes along which aromatics, valuables, and other goods moved toward the Mediterranean. [[incense-trade|Incense trade]] depended on intermediaries as well as the places that grew or processed the products. The use of a South Arabian name in a distant source can reflect a connection with such a network without implying a direct political relationship. Northern Arabian traders and communities also participated, and biblical names may reflect more than one setting.

## In the Bible

Gen 10:7, 28 places forms of the name in two lists, linking Sheba with different lines of descent. The similar name Seba is also present in Gen 10:7. Isa 43:3 and 45:14 locate Seba in company with African peoples. The names should be distinguished before geographical conclusions are drawn. Job 1:15 and 6:19 use names associated with Sabeans or Sheba in a narrative and poetic setting closer to routes or groups in northern Arabia. Their exact relationship to the kingdom at Marib remains open.

First Kings 10:1–13 depicts the queen bringing spices, gold, and stones and testing [[solomon|Solomon]]’s wisdom. No known Sabaean record confirms her visit. Some scholars see the narrative as preserving an earlier memory of contact. Others emphasize the fuller evidence for trade in later centuries and the account’s literary role in displaying Solomon’s wisdom and reach. Ps 72:10, 15 and Isa 60:6 also evoke gifts and aromatics from Sheba. Jer 6:20 refers to frankincense from there, and Ezek 27:22–23 connects Sheba with a wider trade network. Matt 12:42 invokes the queen as a figure who traveled to hear wisdom. Much later, Ethiopia’s *Kebra Nagast* developed the queen’s visit into a story of dynastic origins.
