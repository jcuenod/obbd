## Overview

Hiram I is the king of Tyre remembered as an ally of David and Solomon in the tenth century BCE. In Samuel, Kings, and Chronicles, he supplies timber and skilled labor and takes part in a maritime venture. Josephus quotes later Greek-language histories that also place Hiram in the Tyrian succession. No known contemporary inscription names him. The evidence gives a picture of how an inland capital and a coastal kingdom could cooperate, while leaving the details of his reign and its chronology uncertain.

## Extrabiblical Sources

In *Against Apion* 1.106–127, [[josephus|Josephus]] quotes Dius and Menander of Ephesus on Hiram, including civic and temple projects at [[tyre|Tyre]]. In *Antiquities* 8.141–149 he discusses the relationship of Hiram and Solomon and appeals to records and historians. The extracts attributed to Menander present Hiram as the son of Abibaal and assign him a reign of thirty-four years. The quoted historians wrote long after the period in question, and their works are known chiefly through later authors. Josephus also had an interest in defending the antiquity of the Jerusalem temple.

The succession and reign lengths attributed to Menander have been used to calculate backward from the foundation of Carthage, placed in the reign of Pygmalion in the Tyrian tradition. Different assumptions about the founder's date and the transmission of reign lengths produce different chronologies. Reconstructions place Hiram's reign approximately in 980–947 or 969–936 BCE. The ranges express competing calculations rather than contemporary dates.

## Biblical Portrayal

In 2 Sam 5:11, Hiram sends cedar and craftspeople for [[david|David's]] palace. In 1 Kgs 5:1–12 (MT 5:15–26) the relationship continues under [[solomon|Solomon]], with a treaty and the supply of cedar and cypress in exchange for provisions. In 1 Kgs 9:10–14 Solomon gives Hiram towns that the latter regards as unsatisfactory. The exchange, associated with the name Cabul, gives a different picture of the alliance from the cooperative arrangement for building.

The projects extend to seafaring. In 1 Kgs 9:26–28, Hiram supplies experienced sailors for Solomon's fleet, which brings gold from [[ophir|Ophir]]. 1 Kgs 10:11, 22 describes further cargoes and associates Hiram's fleet with ships of [[tarshish|Tarshish]], a term with debated implications for vessel type and destination. The stories give a literary picture of specialized [[phoenician-seafaring|Phoenician seafaring]] in the service of royal exchange.

In 2 Chr 2:3–16 (MT 2:2–15), the king appears as Huram. He sends a skilled artisan called Huram-abi. The craftsman called Hiram in 1 Kgs 7:13–14 is likewise from Tyre and works in bronze. The royal sender and the artisan are distinct people.

## Historical Assessment

The narrative placement of Hiram's help to David relatively early in David's reign makes it hard to fit a single thirty-four-year reign comfortably around all the traditions. The sequence of episodes in Samuel need not fix a precise year for the palace. Excavations at Tyre give only a partial view of the tenth-century city, and several of the clearest records of Phoenician expansion toward Cyprus and farther west belong to later periods. The biblical accounts and the later Tyrian traditions preserve a picture of cooperation, while the scale and date of particular projects remain open.
