## Overview

Ramesses II was the third pharaoh of Egypt's Nineteenth Dynasty and ruled for about sixty-six years, conventionally dated c. 1279–1213 BCE. His inscriptions commemorate a battle with the Hittites at Kadesh, a subsequent peace treaty and a diplomatic marriage. He built on a vast scale and developed Pi-Ramesses in the eastern Nile Delta. The biblical place name Rameses has encouraged the identification of Ramesses II with the pharaoh of the exodus. The narrative leaves the king anonymous, and his extensive monuments give no direct evidence for the identification.

## Extrabiblical Sources

Colossal statues, temple reliefs, building inscriptions and diplomatic texts document his reign. He erected or enlarged structures at Abu Simbel, Luxor and other cult centers and built the Ramesseum, his memorial temple at Thebes. At [[pi-ramesses|Pi-Ramesses]], on the eastern Delta near earlier Avaris, Ramesses developed a royal residence on a site already used by his predecessors. Its eastern position was useful for military campaigns and communications with Egypt's Levantine territories. [@ael, 2:57–72] [@anep, figs. 333, 336–337]

In his fifth regnal year, c. 1274 BCE, Ramesses confronted Muwatalli II of Hatti at Kadesh on the Orontes. The [[kadesh-battle-reliefs|Egyptian reliefs and texts]] portray him as the hero after a Hittite attack broke into the Egyptian camp. Kadesh remained in the Hittite sphere. In year 21, c. 1259 BCE, Ramesses and Hattusili III concluded a [[egyptian-hittite-peace-treaty|treaty]] guaranteeing peace and providing for mutual assistance and extradition. The Egyptian texts and the Akkadian versions from Hattusa record undertakings exchanged by the two courts. A later [[diplomatic-marriage|marriage]] to a daughter of Hattusili further consolidated the relationship. [@ael, 2:57–72] [@beckman-hdt, 90–94]

## Biblical Portrayal

Exod 1:11 names Raamses and Pithom as store cities built under forced labor. Exod 12:37 begins the journey to Succoth at Rameses. Gen 47:11 names the land of Rameses as the region where Joseph settled his family. The forms of the name point toward the Delta residence and its district. Its appearance in Genesis illustrates how an account of an earlier period can locate events with a place name familiar to later readers. The chronological implications of the place name in Exodus are therefore debated. [@abd, 5:618–619]

## Historical Assessment

Scholars who propose a thirteenth-century exodus can place it within the period when Pi-Ramesses flourished. Other accounts of the narrative's formation allow for later use of an older name or for remembered details to be brought together in the story. The [[merneptah|Merneptah Stele]] places a group called Israel in Canaan by the late thirteenth century BCE, after Ramesses II's reign. It supplies a terminus for that attestation of Israel and leaves its connection to the [[exodus-historical-questions|exodus tradition]] open. Ramesses II's mummy supplies no evidence of drowning. The identification became familiar to modern audiences through *The Ten Commandments* and *The Prince of Egypt*. [@abd, 5:618–619]
