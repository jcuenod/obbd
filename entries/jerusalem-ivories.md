## Overview

Excavations in the Givʿati Parking Lot in Jerusalem uncovered fragments of decorated ivory panels from Building 100, a large late Iron Age building. The panels had probably been attached to furniture or other luxury furnishings. They were recovered in a destruction deposit associated with the Babylonian conquest of Jerusalem. The finds give material evidence for the kind of elite furnishings evoked by the ivory couches of Amos and the ivory decoration of Psalm 45. The panels' original arrangement and the furniture to which they belonged remain uncertain.

## Description and Provenance

Building 100 stands on the western side of the [[city-of-david|City of David]] ridge. Its excavators recovered the pieces from debris in the building and published a preliminary report in 2022. The fragments preserve carved decorative designs, including rosettes, plant forms, and patterned borders. Their use as inlays is inferred from the form and finish of the pieces. The surviving fragments are too incomplete to assign securely to a particular bed, couch, or other object [@avisar-jerusalem-ivories, 57–74].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Amos 6:1, 4 directs its criticism at the secure and wealthy, especially the elite of Samaria, and uses reclining on ivory beds to evoke their manner of life. The Jerusalem assemblage supplies evidence for a comparable use of prestige materials in Judah. The [[samaria-ivories]] provide a separate, more local comparison for the northern kingdom.

Ps 45:8 (MT 45:9) associates ivory with the interior of royal palaces. Ivory panels show how a costly material could ornament furnishings without being the material from which the whole object was made. The throne in 1 Kgs 10:18 combines ivory and gold in the description of Solomon's court. The Givʿati finds belong to a much later archaeological context and provide a comparison for luxury craft.

## Scholarly Issues

The destruction context places the panels in a late phase of Judahite elite life. Identifying a particular owner, workshop, or original piece of furniture requires more evidence. The designs also belong to a wider decorative repertoire, so comparison with Samaria or other regional centers can help characterize the finds without making all the panels the work of one workshop.
