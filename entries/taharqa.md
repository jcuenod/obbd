## Overview

Taharqa, called Tirhakah in Kings and Isaiah, was a Kushite ruler of Egypt's Twenty-fifth Dynasty. His monuments record building work and religious patronage in Egypt and Nubia, and Assyrian royal inscriptions recount his wars with Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal. The biblical account names him when Sennacherib receives news of an approaching Kushite force. The generally accepted date of Sennacherib's campaign against Judah, 701 BCE, precedes Taharqa's accession by about a decade. The reference therefore raises questions about his possible service before becoming king and the chronology of the biblical narrative.

## Extrabiblical Sources

Taharqa ruled approximately 690–664 BCE. His inscriptions from Kawa record temple patronage and recall his coming north to Egypt under an earlier king. Building survives at sites including Kawa in Nubia and Karnak in Egypt. His monuments frame his reign as the continuation and renewal of cult at important centers in both regions. The pyramid at Nuri associated with him belongs to a different body of evidence for the dynasty's power and burial practices [@abd, 4:109–111].

Sennacherib's account of his third campaign describes fighting Egyptian kings and forces of the king of Kush at Eltekeh. It does not give Taharqa's name. Esarhaddon's inscriptions later name Taharqa as king of Egypt and Kush. In 671 BCE Esarhaddon captured Memphis and reports taking members of the royal household. Taharqa escaped and subsequently recovered Memphis. Ashurbanipal's annals describe a renewed Assyrian expedition and Taharqa's retreat. These records tell the story from the standpoint of Assyrian royal victory [@rinap3-1, no. 22 ii 37–iii 49; @rinap4, no. 98; @anet, 287–288].

## Biblical Portrayal

In 2 Kgs 19:9 and the parallel in Isa 37:9, Sennacherib hears that Tirhakah king of Cush has come out to fight against him. The report intervenes in the narrative of Assyrian pressure on Jerusalem. Earlier the Assyrian envoy dismisses Egypt as an unreliable source of military support (2 Kgs 18:21). The biblical account identifies a potential adversary of Sennacherib independently of Judah and places that threat in the midst of the exchange with Hezekiah.

## Historical Assessment

The combination of Assyrian and biblical evidence makes Egyptian and Kushite involvement in the 701 campaign secure. The date of Taharqa's accession makes the title “king of Cush” difficult to read as a contemporary designation for him in 701. Some scholars see a retrospective title applied to a royal prince or military commander. The Kawa inscriptions attest his movement north before his reign but provide no secure link to Eltekeh. Others propose a second campaign by Sennacherib or a literary combination of traditions from separate encounters. A later Sennacherib campaign against Judah lacks contemporary confirmation. The presence of Kushite forces, Taharqa's participation, and the dating of any participation are consequently distinct historical questions.
