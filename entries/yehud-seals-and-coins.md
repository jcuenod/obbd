## Overview

Yehud stamp impressions and coins name Judah as a province or place under Persian and early Hellenistic rule. The stamps were impressed on storage jars, and some name officials. The coins are small silver issues, many with legends in Paleo-Hebrew. Together they attest aspects of administration and the circulation of goods that biblical narratives mention less directly. The distribution of excavated jars provides secure archaeological evidence. Identifications of individuals on seals or coins with biblical figures depend on chronology, provenance and the reading of particular inscriptions.

## Archaeological Evidence

The stamped jar handles bear forms of the name Yehud such as *yhd* and *yhwd*. Some impressions also include personal names and the Aramaic title *pḥwʾ*, “the governor.” Lipschits and Vanderhooft distinguish early, middle and late types using script, spelling, formula and archaeological context. The early and middle types occur especially often at [[ramat-rahel|Ramat Rahel]] and Jerusalem. Finds from the Mizpah area and other sites help trace the distribution of jars, and later types show the continuation of stamping under Hellenistic rule. Aramaic script is common, with some Paleo-Hebrew forms and mixed features. [@neaehl] [@avigad-sass-wsss]

Small silver [[coinage|coins]] of Yehud began to appear in the late Persian period, and local issues continued into the early Ptolemaic period. Most use [[paleo-hebrew-script|Paleo-Hebrew]] lettering. Some designs adapt types familiar from Athenian silver, including the owl. A local legend could therefore identify a coin's issuing community while its imagery drew on a design circulating much more widely. The series includes types naming Yehezqiyah the governor and Yohanan the priest. Chronology at the transition from Persian to Macedonian rule is disputed. An unusual drachm in the British Museum depicts a seated figure on a winged wheel. Its short inscription has been read as *yhd*, Yehud, or *yhw*, a divine name. The choice affects the interpretation, while the image leaves the figure's identity open. [@abd] [@keel-uehlinger-ggig]

## In the Bible

Ezra 5:8 supplies the Aramaic name [[yehud-province|Yehud]] in a reference to the province. Hag 1:1, 14 calls Zerubbabel its governor, Neh 5:14–15 distinguishes Nehemiah from earlier governors, and Mal 1:8 assumes the governor's high standing. The stamped titles provide comparative evidence for this office. They give no secure stamp of any of these biblical individuals. [@tad, A4.7] [@avigad-sass-wsss] [@abd]

The priest Yohanan on the coin has been identified with Johanan in the high priestly family associated with Neh 12:10–11, 22. The recurring name and uncertain date of the issue make the identification tentative. A seal naming Shelomith in relation to Elnathan the governor and seal impressions bearing Elnathan's name are unprovenanced. A suggestion that this Shelomith is Zerubbabel's daughter named in 1 Chr 3:19 remains conjectural. The proposed identifications are more specific claims than the use of the objects to illustrate official titles and names. [@avigad-sass-wsss] [@abd]
