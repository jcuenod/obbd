## Overview

Households in ancient Israel and Judah could be settings for ritual, family sacrifice, and efforts to obtain protection. Biblical narratives describe images and ritual specialists associated with particular households, while prophetic texts describe family participation in worship they condemn. Archaeology adds objects from residential settings, some of whose purposes remain uncertain. Taken together, the evidence shows that religious activity extended beyond major sanctuaries and that families could participate through work done at home.

## Ancient Near Eastern Evidence

Small figurines and amulets occur in residential and other settings. Some may have been used to seek protection or well-being, but the place of recovery and the object's form do not always disclose its function. A [[judean-pillar-figurines|Judahite pillar figurine]], for example, need not have the same use as another figurine of similar shape. The distribution and condition of the finds matter alongside their appearance [@nakhai-israelite-religion] [@keel-uehlinger-ggig].

Mesopotamian evidence for childbirth and infant care includes protective practices associated with the home. It gives examples of domestic ritual from another region. It cannot by itself identify the protective objects or practices of an Israelite household [@flynn-children].

## In the Hebrew Bible

In Gen 31:19, 30–35 Rachel takes Laban's [[teraphim]]. The episode treats them as valued portable objects, and Rachel hides them during the search. It leaves their precise function and any connection to inheritance unstated. Judg 17:1–13 describes Micah setting up a shrine with images and employing a Levite. The account presents specific private arrangements for personnel and space rather than a general description of domestic worship.

In 1 Sam 20:6, 29 a family sacrifice in Bethlehem is invoked to explain David's absence from the royal meal. Jer 7:17–18 assigns separate work to children, fathers, and women in preparing offerings to the [[queen-of-heaven]]. In Jer 44:15–19 the speakers defend the practice. The prophetic context is one of dispute, but the description makes the contribution of different household members visible.

## Interpretive Significance

Domestic ritual encompassed actions of different kinds and scales. A family sacrifice, protection sought for a child, and a private shrine should each be understood in its own context. Archaeological evidence can establish the presence of ritual objects or suggest patterns of use, while narratives and prophetic arguments give distinct kinds of evidence for how household participation was understood.
