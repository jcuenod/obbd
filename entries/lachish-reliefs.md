## Overview

The Lachish Reliefs are carved stone panels from the palace of the Assyrian king Sennacherib at Nineveh, in modern Iraq. Made in the early seventh century BCE, they portray the Assyrian siege and capture of the fortified Judahite city of Lachish in 701 BCE. They show the attack on the city walls, the forced removal of residents, and the presentation of captured goods to the king. The carvings give an Assyrian court's view of an event also described in Kings and Chronicles, where Lachish is the base from which Sennacherib sends envoys to Jerusalem.

## Description and Provenance

The scenes are carved in low relief, with figures projecting slightly from the surface of gypsum wall slabs. The slabs lined a room in the Southwest Palace of [[sennacherib|Sennacherib]] at [[nineveh|Nineveh]], where Austen Henry Layard uncovered them in the mid-nineteenth century. <!-- VERIFY: exact excavation season for the Lachish room; the assigned date range and secondary accounts differ. --> The main panels are now in the British Museum in London. An Akkadian caption in cuneiform, the wedge-shaped writing of Assyria, names Sennacherib and Lachish and identifies the enthroned king as the one before whom the city's spoils are presented.

## Contents

The assault scenes show archers, defenders on the walls, and covered battering rams, machines used to strike walls or gates. The rams approach the defenses on sloping ramps, a feature of [[assyrian-siege-warfare|Assyrian siege warfare]]. Other scenes show families leaving with their belongings, soldiers carrying off goods, and the [[treatment-of-captives|punishment of captives]], including impalement and flaying. The series leads the viewer from the fighting to the seated king, before whom prisoners and spoils appear [@anep].

Excavations at [[lachish|Lachish]] give a separate line of evidence for the assault. David Ussishkin's excavations investigated the destruction of Level III, the archaeological layer associated with the capture of 701 BCE. At the point of attack, the remains include an Assyrian siege ramp and a counter-ramp, a mass of material piled on the inside to reinforce the defenses. Numerous arrowheads and sling stones were also found. These finds support the picture of a major assault, but not every detail in the carvings can be matched to excavated remains [@neaehl].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

In 2 Kgs 18:14, 17, Hezekiah sends a message to Sennacherib at Lachish, and the Assyrian king sends officers from Lachish to Jerusalem. The narrative later places Sennacherib at Libnah after he leaves Lachish (2 Kgs 19:8). Second Chronicles explicitly places him at the siege when he sends his servants to Jerusalem (2 Chr 32:9). The reliefs depict the capture of the same city but do not show those exchanges or the move to Libnah. Mic 1:13 addresses Lachish in a lament over Judah's towns without narrating the siege.

The scenes illuminate the equipment in Ezekiel's portrayals of a siege of Jerusalem, including mounds and battering rams (Ezek 4:2; 21:22 [MT 21:27]). They provide material background for that imagery rather than illustrations of Ezekiel's words. The departing families also give a visual counterpart to the Assyrian envoy's promise to take Jerusalem's inhabitants to “a land like your own” (2 Kgs 18:32, NIV). The speech presents removal as an inducement to surrender, while the carvings show [[deportation|forced resettlement]] as a consequence of conquest.

## Scholarly Issues

The reliefs are royal propaganda: they arrange the story to display Assyrian power and the king's command over the outcome. The [[sennacherib-annals|royal annals]] describe Hezekiah confined in Jerusalem but do not claim that the capital fell [@rinap3-1]. Lachish may have been chosen for such an extensive display because it was the most important Judahite city actually captured in the [[sennacherib-campaign-701|campaign of 701 BCE]], but the palace caption does not explain the choice. The carvings can therefore preserve evidence about the campaign while giving a selective account of it.
