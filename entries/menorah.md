## Overview

The menorah is the lampstand associated with the tabernacle and the Jewish temple. The book of Exodus describes a gold stand with six branches and seven lamps, decorated with cups shaped like almond blossoms. Other biblical passages describe different arrangements of sanctuary lighting. Late Second Temple coins, the Magdala Stone, and the Arch of Titus preserve images of a seven-lamp form. The textual and visual evidence helps readers distinguish particular lampstands and understand how their light became an image of divine presence and communal identity.

## Archaeological Evidence

Stylized trees with balanced branches occur in Bronze Age Levantine art. Carol Meyers drew on this imagery to interpret the tabernacle lampstand's branches, cups, buds, and flowers. The comparison gives the design a setting in ancient art and helps explain its appearance as a plant. It does not fix the date when the description in Exodus was written [@anep] [@keel-symbolism].

Coins issued by Mattathias Antigonus in the late first century BCE show a menorah and are among the earliest securely dated images. The [[magdala-stone|Magdala Stone]] depicts one in a first-century synagogue. The [[arch-of-titus|Arch of Titus]] in Rome, erected after Titus's death in 81 CE, shows a menorah carried with other temple spoils. The broad stepped base shown there differs from the bases in some Jewish representations. Scholars debate whether it depicts the original base, a support used to carry the object, or a feature of the Roman representation.

## In the Bible

Exod 25:31–40 describes a gold lampstand of hammered work with six branches and a central shaft. Exod 37:17–24 describes its construction. The botanical ornament frames seven [[oil-lamps|lamps]], and Exod 27:20–21 and Lev 24:1–4 establish regular tending and oil provision for the light. The instructions belong to the Priestly presentation of the [[tabernacle]]. In 1 Sam 3:3, the lamp of God burns at Shiloh, and its shape is unspecified.

1 Kgs 7:49 places ten gold lampstands in Solomon's temple, with five on either side. 2 Chr 13:11 speaks of a gold lampstand in describing service in Judah. The passages differ in detail and purpose. Zech 4:1–14 presents a vision of a lampstand with a bowl, seven lamps, and two olive trees. Its form has been compared with excavated lamps with several spouts, and the vision adds imagery of its own. It need not depict the same design as Exodus.

1 Macc 1:21 and 4:49–50 report the removal of a lampstand and its replacement at the temple rededication. The references place the lampstand within the continuity of worship despite the loss of particular furnishings. Rev 1:12–13, 20 depicts seven golden lampstands and identifies them as churches. The vision adapts sanctuary imagery to express the setting in which Christ stands among the congregations.
