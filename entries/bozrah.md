## Overview

Bozrah was an important center of ancient Edom, usually identified with Busayra in the highlands of southern Jordan. Excavations revealed monumental buildings from the latter part of the Iron Age, when the kings of Edom are also attested in Assyrian records. Genesis names Bozrah as the home of an Edomite king, while Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Amos use the city as a focus for judgment on Edom. The site's remains help explain why the city could stand for Edomite power, while leaving questions about its earliest history open.

## Location and Identification

Busayra lies on a defensible ridge in the southern Jordanian highlands. The likeness of the name and the location within Edom, together with the scale of the archaeological remains, support the usual identification with biblical Bozrah. The later city called Bostra in southern Syria is a different site. The evidence for Busayra also comes from a period well before the Roman prominence of Bostra [@abd].

## Archaeological History

Crystal Bennett excavated at Busayra between 1971 and 1980. Her team uncovered substantial buildings on the upper fortified part of the settlement, including rooms arranged around open spaces. Building plans and details have been compared with Assyrian and Syrian examples. The comparisons suggest contact with architectural traditions of the wider region, although the buildings' functions and the direction of influence are matters of interpretation.

The main excavated occupation begins in the later Iron Age, with monumental phases in the late eighth through sixth centuries BCE and continuing use into later periods. Earlier evidence at the site is limited. Inscriptions of Tiglath-pileser III and Esarhaddon independently name rulers of [[edomites|Edom]], including Qaus-malaka and Qaus-gabri, respectively. They show the kingdom within the sphere of [[assyria|Assyrian]] power, although those notices do not specify that the named rulers resided at Bozrah [@rinap1] [@rinap4].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Gen 36:33 presents Jobab son of Zerah of Bozrah as one of the kings of Edom. The late flourishing of Busayra raises questions about when the list took shape and which periods its traditions remember. Work at [[faynan|Faynan]] shows substantial activity in a neighboring part of the region in earlier centuries. That evidence bears on the origins of political organization in Edom without dating the kings of Genesis or the foundation of Bozrah directly.

Amos 1:12 describes fire consuming Bozrah's strongholds. Jer 49:13, 22 pictures the city desolate and threatened by attack. The references are intelligible in light of Busayra's emergence as an important center, but archaeological dates for its buildings cannot establish the composition dates of these passages.

Isa 34:6 depicts slaughter in Edom as a sacrifice at Bozrah. In Isa 63:1 the victorious figure comes from Bozrah with dyed clothing, in a scene that develops the image of the [[divine-warrior|divine warrior]]. Bozrah represents Edom in the imagery of both passages. The scenes use its prominence to give a geographic focus to their depictions of judgment.
