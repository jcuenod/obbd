## Overview

Late Iron Age seal impressions from Jerusalem preserve the names of officials who lived around the time of Jeremiah. Archaeologists use the term bulla for a lump of clay stamped with a seal, often attached to a tied document. Several excavated examples name people and fathers also named in Jeremiah. Other objects with biblical names came through antiquities markets and present different problems of dating and authenticity. The strongest comparisons use the wording of the impression together with its archaeological context and the biblical setting.

## Description and Provenance

A [[seals-and-bullae|seal impression]] could preserve the name or title of an official connected with a document or parcel. Some impressions retain traces of cords and writing material on their backs. Yigal Shiloh's excavation on the [[city-of-david|City of David ridge]] recovered about fifty bullae from a building destroyed in the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem in 587 or 586 BCE. Fire hardened the clay, while the documents themselves did not survive [@neaehl] [@avigad-sass-wsss].

Later City of David excavations led by Eilat Mazar yielded sealings naming people also mentioned in Jeremiah. A separate excavation at the Givati Parking Lot produced a bulla with the name Nathan-melech followed by the title “servant of the king.” A substantial group of other Hebrew bullae was published by Nahman Avigad from a collection without recorded excavation contexts. These include impressions attributed to Baruch and Jerahmeel, whose contexts cannot be independently reconstructed [@avigad-sass-wsss].

## Contents

One bulla from Shiloh's excavations names Gemaryahu son of Shaphan. Mazar's excavations produced impressions naming Yehukal son of Shelemyahu and Gedalyahu son of Pashhur. The second element in each case is a patronym, meaning a name identifying the person's father. A matching patronym makes an identification more specific than an isolated personal name. The Yehukal impression adds the grandfather's name, which the biblical passages do not supply.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

In Jer 36:10–12, 25 the scroll is read in the chamber of Gemariah son of Shaphan, and Gemariah later opposes its destruction. Jer 37:3 and 38:1 name Jehucal son of Shelemiah, while 38:1 also names Gedaliah son of Pashhur. The combinations of personal name, father's name, period, and place make these identifications strong. The bullae do not describe the episodes involving the people they name. Jer 32:10–14 provides separate evidence for the practice of [[sealed-deeds|sealing a legal document]].

The excavated Nathan-melech impression plausibly relates to the official of the same name in 2 Kgs 23:11, but the two sources use different terms for the office. The Gedaliah bulla from Lachish has a title and no father's name, making identification with Gedaliah son of Ahikam in 2 Kgs 25:22 uncertain.

## Scholarly Issues

The two impressions attributed to Baruch have a name, father's name, and title that correspond to Jer 36:4, 32. Neither has an excavated context. Yuval Goren and Eran Arie concluded that the objects are probably modern products after examining their material and construction. A published rejoinder challenged their conclusions, but the bullae remain insecure evidence for the biblical scribe. The similarly unprovenanced impression related to Jerahmeel in Jer 36:26 also warrants caution.
