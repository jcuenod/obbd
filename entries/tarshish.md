## Overview

Tarshish is a biblical name associated with long sea voyages and with the metal trade of Tyre. Ezekiel names silver, iron, tin, and lead among its goods. Jonah boards a ship for Tarshish to flee from his commission, and Isaiah uses the name for a distant people. Proposed identifications include Tartessos in southwestern Iberia and a location in Sardinia. The evidence points to a broad maritime horizon but leaves the exact location disputed. The expression “ships of Tarshish” may also designate vessels capable of long voyages, including vessels intended for the route to Ophir.

## Location and Identification

A western Mediterranean location fits the distance and trading associations of several biblical passages. Tartessos is attractive because of its name, position, and access to metal-producing regions. Sardinia has been proposed in view of its mineral resources and early Phoenician contacts. The Phoenician inscription from Nora, Sardinia, contains letters read by some editors as the name Tarshish. Their grouping and relation to the inscription's setting remain disputed. Cilician Tarsus has also been proposed on the basis of the name, although the western maritime evidence provides a different geographical context [@kai; @abd].

An inscription of Esarhaddon names Tarsisi alongside other territory in a statement about rulers of the sea. The usual comparison with Tarshish is plausible. Its inclusion in the text does not fix a harbor on a modern map, and the inscription is a statement of royal reach [@rinap4].

## Archaeological History

Phoenician settlements and imported goods document travel and exchange with both Sardinia and the Iberian peninsula. Metal supplies also moved through networks that included local producers and several ports. Isotopic work on silver from southern Phoenician hoards finds signatures consistent with ore sources in both regions. The source of an ore and the trading place where silver was acquired can differ. The analyses strengthen the general picture of western exchange while leaving the name of any particular port open.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Ezek 27:12 lists metals from Tarshish in its account of Tyre's commerce, and Jer 10:9 identifies Tarshish with wrought silver. The oracles against Tyre in Isa 23:1–14 picture the disruption of trade through the ships of Tarshish and the coastal world that supported them. Isa 2:16 includes such ships among impressive things brought low. The commercial language helps explain why the ships could stand for wealth and reach.

The fleet in 1 Kgs 10:22 is associated with costly goods. In 1 Kgs 22:48, Jehoshaphat's ships of Tarshish are intended for Ophir but wreck at Ezion-geber before departure. That intended destination favors reading the expression in at least some contexts as a class or capacity of vessel. In Jonah 1:3 Tarshish is the destination named at Joppa. The notice emphasizes departure by sea and distance from the commission. Gen 10:4 locates Tarshish among the descendants of Javan, and Isa 66:19 places it among distant recipients of a message.
