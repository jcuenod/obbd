## Overview

Ezion-geber is a place near the head of the Gulf of Aqaba in biblical narratives about wilderness travel and voyages on the Red Sea. Kings says that Solomon built ships there and that a later fleet commissioned by Jehoshaphat was wrecked there. The location of the ancient port remains uncertain. Tell el-Kheleifeh, a nearby Iron Age site, is a leading candidate in the history of research, but reassessment of its remains weakened claims that it was Solomon's port or a large copper refinery. The evidence clarifies the geography of the narratives while leaving the history of the expeditions open.

## Location and Identification

The texts locate Ezion-geber near Elath, also spelled Eloth, on the coast at the southern end of the Arabah valley. Tell el-Kheleifeh, at the head of the gulf, was proposed as Ezion-geber in the 1930s. It may instead represent Elath or a later fortified settlement close to the shore. Jeziret Faraʿun, an island with a protected anchorage farther down the western side of the gulf, has also been proposed. The biblical descriptions establish the general region with more confidence than the exact site [@pratico-kheleifeh, 1–5] [@abd, 2:723–727].

## Archaeological History

Nelson Glueck excavated Tell el-Kheleifeh between 1938 and 1940. He initially identified its principal early building as a copper refinery from the time of Solomon. He dropped the refinery interpretation after reconsidering openings he had taken for flues. Such openings can be explained as part of the building's construction, and the complex has been interpreted in light of fortified and storage architecture.

Gary D. Pratico's reappraisal of the excavated material dates the securely documented occupation mainly to the eighth through fourth centuries BCE. It identified no clear tenth-century occupation. Evidence of later material connections with [[edomites|Edom]] includes handmade pottery of a type found in the Negev and a jar impression naming Qosʿanal as a servant of the king. These [[edomite-inscriptions|Edomite inscriptions]] help characterize the settlement without identifying it as Solomon's port [@pratico-kheleifeh, 21–25].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Num 33:35–36 places Ezion-geber on a route to the wilderness of Zin and Kadesh. Deut 2:8 names it alongside Elath on the Arabah road. The two places are again associated in Kings, which reports that Solomon built a fleet at Ezion-geber and received help from Hiram's men to bring gold from [[ophir|Ophir]] (1 Kgs 9:26–28). Chronicles likewise locates the places in Edom (2 Chr 8:17–18). The location would suit a Red Sea voyage. The scale and historical date of the venture remain debated.

1 Kgs 22:48 (MT 22:49) places the wreck of Jehoshaphat's ships at Ezion-geber, and 2 Chr 20:35–37 describes a venture involving Ahaziah. Later accounts portray control of Elath as contested. Azariah restores it to Judah in 2 Kgs 14:22. In 2 Kgs 16:6, the written Hebrew has Rezin restoring Elath to Aram, with a traditional reading that identifies those who settled there as Edomites. Some modern translations adopt Edom in the earlier clause as well. The textual difficulty limits how securely the verse can be used to reconstruct a change of rule.
