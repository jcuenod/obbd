## Overview

The Bubastite Portal at Karnak in Egypt preserves a relief made for Shoshenq I, founder of the Twenty-second Dynasty. It associates the pharaoh's victory with a list of places in the southern Levant. The monument is usually linked with the campaign of Shishak described in Kings and Chronicles around the late tenth century BCE. It gives independent evidence for Egyptian activity in the region, including places beyond Judah. The Egyptian list and the biblical accounts have different purposes, and the relationship between particular details remains a subject of study.

## Description and Provenance

The portal gives access to the first court of the temple of Amun at Karnak. The victory scene is carved on the adjoining temple wall. It combines a royal figure striking enemies with the god Amun presenting rows of bound figures. The relief belongs to a long Egyptian convention of representing enemies and places under divine and royal control. Damage has removed parts of the composition [@anet] [@anep].

## Contents

The small figures in the rows carry oval rings containing the names of places. Recognizable or commonly proposed names include [[megiddo|Megiddo]], Beth-shean, and places farther south near Judah and the Negev. Other names are damaged or difficult to locate. An inscribed fragment of a separate monument bearing Shoshenq's name was found at Megiddo, confirming that a royal monument was present there. The fragment does not establish the circumstances in which it was erected [@neaehl].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

In 1 Kgs 14:25–26 Shishak marches against Jerusalem in Rehoboam's fifth year and takes treasures from the temple and palace. 2 Chr 12:2–9 expands the account, including a notice that Shishak took fortified towns in Judah. No secure identification of Jerusalem occurs in the surviving Egyptian list. Suggestions include loss of the name through damage, omission because Jerusalem gave up treasure without being stormed, and a campaign in which the city itself was outside the Egyptian action. The preservation and genre of the list leave those proposals open.

Josh 17:11 names Beth-shean, Taanach, and Megiddo, places often identified in the Egyptian list. A possible reading of Penuel would also connect the list with a town said to have been fortified by Jeroboam in 1 Kgs 12:25. A common name across the texts supplies a geographic point of comparison. It cannot itself tell how the Egyptians approached a town or prove that they destroyed it.

## Scholarly Issues

Some scholars arrange the name rings into an itinerary, using clusters of places to reconstruct a campaign route. Kevin Wilson and others have emphasized the way Egyptian artists organized similar lists by visual or scribal convention. The surviving arrangement allows several proposed routes. An isolated and uncertain name once read as the Field of Abram provides too little evidence for a historical conclusion.

The identification of Shishak with [[shoshenq-i|Shoshenq I]] is widely accepted and provides a link between Egyptian and biblical chronologies. A date around 925 BCE is conventional and depends in part on the chronology of Egypt's Third Intermediate Period. Proposed links between the campaign and destruction layers at excavated sites have also entered the [[low-chronology-debate|debate over Iron Age chronology]], but the monument rarely supplies enough information to identify a specific destruction layer [@mazar-archaeology].
