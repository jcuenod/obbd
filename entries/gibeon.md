## Overview

Gibeon was an ancient town in the hills northwest of Jerusalem, identified with the present village of el-Jib. Inscribed jar handles bearing the town's name, a large rock-cut pool, and other water installations document its Iron Age history. Storage cavities cut into bedrock point to wine production and storage on a considerable scale. In the Bible, Gibeon is the town that negotiates an agreement with Joshua, a setting for conflict during David's rise, and the site of a sanctuary where Solomon worships. The archaeological finds illuminate the town behind these different accounts.

## Location and Identification

El-Jib occupies a hill in the Benjamin region, near routes connecting Jerusalem with other settlements to its north and west. The similarity of the modern place name and the ancient geography led scholars to identify it with Gibeon. Excavations conducted by James B. Pritchard between 1956 and 1962 recovered jar handles inscribed with the Hebrew name of the town, some also bearing personal names. Finds of this kind establish the identification more securely than the resemblance of place names alone. [@neaehl, 2:511–514] [@dobbs-allsopp-hebrew-inscriptions, 167–180]

## Archaeological History

Among the major remains are a circular pool cut into bedrock, with a stair that winds down the side, and a separate stepped tunnel toward the spring. These [[cisterns-and-water-systems|water installations]] show a substantial investment in making water accessible. Their phases and exact dates within the Iron Age remain debated. The inscribed handles found in the fill help date material thrown into the pool and give only indirect evidence for when it was cut. [@neaehl, 2:511–514]

Excavators also found cavities cut into the rock and interpreted many as storage facilities for jars. The concentration of these installations and the vessel fragments support the interpretation that wine was produced or stored on a substantial scale. The associated names may have identified people responsible for particular jars. An Egyptian place-name in the list of Shoshenq I at Karnak is commonly identified as Gibeon. The Late Bronze evidence includes tomb material, but the excavations did not establish a settlement of the scale suggested in Joshua. [@king-stager-life, 215–222] [@neaehl, 2:511–514] [@anet, 263–264]

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Josh 9 presents Gibeon's leaders as obtaining an agreement by claiming to have come from far away. In Josh 10:1–14, other rulers attack Gibeon in consequence of the agreement, and Joshua comes to its aid. The burial finds indicate some activity in the Late Bronze period. The relationship of the stories to the settlement history remains uncertain. They are evidence for how later biblical writers explained Gibeon's relationship with Israel. [@abd, 2:1010–1013]

The pool is a landmark in 2 Sam 2:12–17, where the opposing forces of Abner and Joab meet. Jer 41:12 similarly places a confrontation by the waters of Gibeon. The excavated installations give a setting for the prominence of water in these narratives, though the exact identification of each location is uncertain. In 1 Kgs 3:4–15, Solomon sacrifices at Gibeon's principal [[high-places|high place]], a local site of worship. No excavated structure has been securely identified as this sanctuary. [@abd, 2:1010–1013] [@neaehl, 2:511–514]
