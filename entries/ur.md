## Overview

Ur was a major city of southern Mesopotamia, on a mound known today as Tell al-Muqayyar in Iraq. It was home to a sanctuary of the moon god and to rulers whose tombs and monumental buildings have made it one of the best-known Mesopotamian sites. Genesis calls Abraham's place of origin Ur of the Chaldeans. The southern city is the usual identification, though some scholars have proposed northern locations. The archaeological evidence documents the city over many centuries and provides context for the place name.

## Location and Identification

Tell al-Muqayyar lies in the southern Iraqi plain near Nasiriyah. Its position in antiquity depended on river channels and waterways that have since shifted. Inscriptions recovered at the site establish its ancient name. The city was associated with [[sin-moon-god|Nanna]], the Sumerian moon god, also known as Sîn. The same deity had an important cult at [[harran|Harran]], another place named in Abraham's journey. This religious link is a feature of the wider history of the two cities.

## Archaeological History

Leonard Woolley's excavations for the British Museum and the University of Pennsylvania ran from 1922 to 1934. They recovered the Royal Cemetery, whose most famous tombs date to around 2600 BCE. The tombs contained richly made objects and in some cases multiple burials. They record the wealth and ceremonies of Early Dynastic elites.

Much later, Ur became the center of a kingdom conventionally called the Third Dynasty of Ur. Ur-Nammu began its best-known monument, the stepped temple tower or [[ziggurat|ziggurat]] associated with Nanna's sanctuary. The buildings underwent changes under later rulers. In the sixth century BCE [[nabonidus|Nabonidus]] repaired sanctuaries and rebuilt the residence associated with the office of high priestess, to which he appointed his daughter Ennigaldi-Nanna.

Woolley also identified a thick deposit left by flooding and proposed comparing it with the biblical flood. It is evidence that flooding affected part of the ancient site. Differences between flood deposits at sites in southern Mesopotamia show that local events must be examined in their own stratigraphic settings.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Gen 11:28, 31 places Haran's death and the family's departure at Ur of the Chaldeans. Gen 15:7 and Neh 9:7 use the place as the beginning of God's call to Abraham. The Greek form of Gen 11:31 refers more generally to the land of the Chaldeans. Acts 7:2–4 places Abraham in Mesopotamia before he settles in Harran and likewise uses the phrase about the Chaldeans' land.

Most scholars identify the biblical place with southern Ur, though proposals for Urfa or a northern place called Ura emphasize the route through Harran. The name “of the Chaldeans” fits the first millennium BCE, when groups called Chaldeans appear in sources concerning southern Babylonia. The designation can reflect the perspective of later writers or readers, even if the narrative situates Abraham much earlier.
