## Overview

The Louvre Leather Roll is an Egyptian administrative document dated to year five of Ramesses II, in the thirteenth century BCE. It records teams' brick-production targets, deliveries, and deficits. The accounts show that Egyptian officials could monitor brick output and record shortfalls. They provide a documentary comparison for the quotas imposed in Exod 5:6–19. Other evidence, including the scribal letters known as the Anastasi papyri, helps illustrate why the supply of straw mattered to brick production.

## Description and Provenance

The roll is inscribed in hieratic, a cursive form of Egyptian script commonly used for accounts. It is held by the Louvre under inventory number E 7976. Its internal regnal date gives a more secure chronological reference than assigning the accounts to a specific building project [@louvre-e7976].

## Contents

Sections of the roll set expected numbers of bricks alongside numbers delivered and amounts outstanding. The repeated entries show how deliveries and shortfalls could be associated with particular groups. The quantity recorded as due should be read as a production target. The document does not securely establish the interval over which each target fell due or the legal status of the workers [@kitchen-brickfields, 141–142].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Exod 5:8 and 13–14 present an unchanged brick quota and punishments when the required output is missed. The leather roll illustrates administrative accounting for targets and shortfalls. It does not describe the particular orders or punishments in Exodus. The [[tomb-of-rekhmire|tomb paintings of Rekhmire]] show stages of the work such accounts presuppose, including molding and handling bricks.

Papyrus Anastasi IV 12.5–13.8 presents a complaint about a lack of brickmakers and straw, with parallel wording in Anastasi V 3.1. Those [[papyrus-anastasi|scribal texts]] and the leather accounts furnish distinct comparisons with Exodus 5. The roll documents the accounting side of production. The letters illustrate problems of labor and raw-material supply.
