## Overview

Menahem son of Gadi seized the throne of Israel in the eighth century BCE and ruled from Samaria. Kings says that he paid silver to secure Assyrian support and raised the payment from wealthy Israelites. Inscriptions of Tiglath-pileser III independently name him among western tributaries. These records establish an important point of contact between Israel and Assyria before Samaria became an Assyrian province. The amount and collection of the payment are reported by Kings, and its precise date remains debated.

## Extrabiblical Sources

The annals of [[tiglath-pileser-iii|Tiglath-pileser III]] name Menahem of [[samaria|Samaria]] among western rulers who gave tribute. The name also appears on a fragmentary stele from Iran, an Assyrian monument recalling tribute and campaigns. These accounts give the Assyrian court's view of the relationship. They do not record the per-person levy reported in Kings. The usual date given for the tribute in the annals is around 738 BCE, with other reconstructions considering earlier payments or earlier activity reflected on the Iran Stele [@rinap1].

## Biblical Portrayal

2 Kgs 15:14–16 depicts Menahem killing Shallum and taking control, then acting violently against Tiphsah and its territory. In 2 Kgs 15:19–20 he pays Pul a thousand talents of silver so that the Assyrian king will support his rule. The payment is raised by a levy on wealthy men, described as fifty shekels apiece. The story makes [[tribute-and-vassalage|tribute]] a means by which a ruler could gain outside backing and impose the cost on his subjects.

Pul is generally identified with Tiglath-pileser III. Babylonian sources use the form Pūlu for him as king of Babylon. In 1 Chr 5:26 the Hebrew wording can sound like a reference to two rulers, since both names appear. The extrabiblical evidence supports identifying the two names with the same ruler [@abc] [@rinap1].

## Historical Assessment

The date of the Assyrian record creates problems for chronologies that end Menahem's reign earlier, including the dates often associated with Edwin Thiele. Some reconstructions distinguish between an initial payment and later tribute. The surviving sources do not establish that every reference describes the same occasion. The royal name in the Assyrian records remains a firm anchor for Menahem's position in the eighth century.

The figures in Kings would imply about sixty thousand contributors if a talent is reckoned at three thousand shekels and both figures are taken literally. This is a calculation from the narrative and should not be treated as a count of landowners. Hos 5:13 and 8:9–10 criticize appeals to Assyria and reliance on foreign partners. They illuminate the broader pressures behind Menahem's policy without identifying his particular payment.
