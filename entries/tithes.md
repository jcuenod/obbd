## Overview

A tithe is a tenth of produce, livestock, or other resources set aside for a recipient. The Bible mentions tithes promised by individuals, claimed by kings, given to sanctuary personnel, shared in celebration, and distributed to people in need. Ugaritic and Babylonian records also document payments assessed as a tenth. Biblical laws assign the tithe to different recipients and give it different uses. Later Jewish texts show people distinguishing several tithes to account for these obligations.

## Ancient Near Eastern Evidence

Administrative texts from [[ugarit|Ugarit]] use a term cognate with the Hebrew word for a tenth. It occurs in documents concerned with payments connected to the palace. In first-millennium Babylonia the Akkadian term *ešrû* occurs in records of temple income. Archives associated with the Eanna temple at Uruk and the Ebabbar temple at Sippar record assessment, collection, and management. The tithe was one way of expressing recurring dues in agricultural economies. The records tell different stories about who had a claim to those dues.

## In the Hebrew Bible

Gen 14:20 describes Abram giving Melchizedek a tenth, and Jacob promises God a tenth at [[bethel|Bethel]] in Gen 28:22. Samuel's warning about a king includes his ability to take a tenth of crops and flocks (1 Sam 8:15, 17). Amos 4:4 mentions tithes in a pointed invitation to worship at Bethel. The references cover both religious and royal claims, and scholars debate their institutional relationship and which developed first.

Lev 27:30–33 assigns a tenth of produce to God and also sets apart every tenth animal passing under a rod. The chapter provides for redeeming part of the tithe of produce with an additional payment. Num 18:21–32 assigns tithes to Levites for their service, with a portion then given to the priests. In Deut 14:22–29 the worshiper eats the tithe before God at the chosen place and may convert it into money for travel. Every third year the produce is stored locally for Levites and for people who may lack support or land. Deut 26:12–15 includes a declaration that these recipients have received their shares.

2 Chr 31:4–12 describes heaps of contributions and arrangements for storing them under Hezekiah. Neh 10:37–39 (MT 10:38–40) assigns collection and storage to Levites and priests, and Neh 13:10–13 recounts renewed efforts to supply the temple personnel. Mal 3:8–10 uses the storehouse to express the community's obligation to provide the full tithe.

## Interpretive Significance

The differences between Numbers and Deuteronomy concern recipients and how the goods were used. Later Jewish writers could read the passages as obligations on different tithes. Tob 1:6–8 distinguishes multiple contributions, including one for Levites, one spent at Jerusalem, and one for other recipients. In Matt 23:23 Jesus's criticism of attention to the tithe on particular herbs is set alongside an appeal to justice, mercy, and faith. The passage assumes detailed practice while distinguishing it from broader obligations.
