## Overview

The Royal Steward Inscription is a Hebrew epitaph carved above the entrance to an elite rock-cut tomb at Silwan, across the Kidron Valley from ancient Jerusalem. It dates to about 700 BCE. The text identifies its owner by the title “who is over the house,” warns that the tomb contains no silver or gold, and curses whoever opens it. The title and the setting recall Isaiah's rebuke of the official Shebna. The damage to the owner's name leaves that identification possible but uncertain.

## Description and Provenance

The three-line inscription was carved into a limestone panel above the doorway. The tomb belongs to a group of monumental cuttings in the bedrock beneath the modern village of Silwan. Their plans and workmanship reflect the architectural distinction available to some of [[jerusalem|Jerusalem]]'s Iron Age elite. Charles Clermont-Ganneau investigated the tomb in 1870. The inscription and a shorter companion text were removed and reached the British Museum, where the longer text is cataloged as BM 125205. Nahman Avigad published the decipherment in 1953. [@neaehl, 2:712–715] [@dobbs-allsopp-hebrew-inscriptions, 507–510]

## Contents

The text names the occupant, of whose name the ending *-yahu* is legible, and gives his office. It then says what is in the tomb. The owner and the woman described by a term commonly understood as “his maidservant” are represented by their bones, and the epitaph warns that there are no precious metals. The precise relationship conveyed by the woman's designation is debated. The final curse targets anyone who opens the tomb. The warning and curse protect the burial by discouraging a search for valuables and threatening consequences for interference. [@kai, no. 191] [@dobbs-allsopp-hebrew-inscriptions, 507–510]

## Significance for Biblical Studies

The [[court-officials|title]] is also applied to Eliakim in 2 Kgs 18:18 and to Ahishar in 1 Kgs 4:6. In Isa 22:15–16, Shebna holds this office and is denounced for carving a tomb in a prominent place. The epitaph supplies independent evidence of a holder of the office commissioning a monumental tomb at Jerusalem. Its closing curse uses the same general formula as the curses on the maker of a secret image in Deut 27:15 and on the rebuilder of Jericho in Josh 6:26. Comparable protections appear on the Phoenician coffins of [[ahiram-sarcophagus|Ahiram]], Tabnit and [[eshmunazar-sarcophagus|Eshmunazar]]. [@abd, 5:129–133, 5:1172–1173] [@kai, nos. 1, 13–14, 191]

## Scholarly Issues

A restoration of the personal name as Shebanyahu, a longer form of Shebna, would suit both the office and the date usually assigned to the lettering. The decisive initial letters are missing, and the ending occurs in many Judahite personal names. The comparison with Isaiah therefore bears most securely on the burial practices and status of a royal steward. The text cannot establish who held the office when the tomb was cut. [@dobbs-allsopp-hebrew-inscriptions, 507–510] [@neaehl, 2:712–715]
