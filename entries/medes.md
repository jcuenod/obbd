## Overview

The Medes were Iranian-speaking peoples of the Zagros region and western Iran. Assyrian inscriptions first describe numerous local rulers. By the end of the seventh century BCE, Median forces had become an important ally of Babylonia in the defeat of Assyria. Cyrus later defeated the Median king Astyages and took Ecbatana. The Bible remembers the Medes as deportation neighbors, opponents of Babylon, and associates of the Persians. These references fit several different periods and need to be distinguished from the sequence of world empires presented in Daniel.

## Sources

Assyrian texts name the Medes from the time of Shalmaneser III and refer to numerous local rulers or city lords. Later Babylonian chronicles document Median rulers and campaigns against Assyria and the defeat of Astyages. Greek accounts, especially Herodotus, give a longer story of dynastic succession beginning with Deioces. They preserve traditions unavailable elsewhere, but their account of a long, centralized Median empire is debated [@rima3] [@rinap1] [@abc].

## History

The [[fall-of-nineveh-chronicle|Babylonian chronicle]] reports that Cyaxares, called Umakištar in Akkadian, led Median forces against Assyria. The Medes took Ashur in 614 BCE, and the Medes and Babylonians cooperated in the capture of Nineveh in 612 BCE. They were capable of substantial coordinated action. Their military successes leave the exact institutions and territorial limits of their polity open.

Around 550 BCE, [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus]] defeated Astyages and took Ecbatana, modern Hamadan. The city remained important under the Achaemenids and is known as a royal residence. [[persia|Persian rule]] incorporated the Median region, and Greek and other sources continued to distinguish Medes from Persians in some settings [@abc] [@glassner-chronicles].

## In the Bible

Gen 10:2 places Madai among the sons of Japheth. 2 Kgs 17:6 names cities of the Medes among places to which Assyria deported Israelites. The passage is consistent with an Assyrian presence in parts of the region in the late eighth century BCE. Isa 13:17 and Jer 51:11, 28 name Medes as opponents of Babylon, and Isa 21:2 uses the commands to Elam and Media in an oracle concerning Babylon. These texts establish the Medes' significance as a recognizable eastern power. The historical circumstances envisaged by each oracle are debated.

Dan 5:28–31 (5:31 = MT 6:1) assigns Babylon's kingdom to the Medes and Persians and introduces [[darius-the-mede|Darius the Mede]]. No contemporary source independently attests that ruler, and Babylon fell to Cyrus without an intervening Median kingdom. Dan 8:20 presents Media and Persia together in the image of a two-horned ram. Dan 6:8 (MT 6:9) and Esth 1:3, 19 use the pairing to express the authority of empire and its law. Ezra 6:2 places a royal document at Ecbatana, a believable setting given the city's role as a Persian royal center.
