## Overview

Sea Peoples is a modern collective label for groups named in Egyptian accounts of warfare around the end of the Late Bronze Age. Merneptah and Ramesses III list partly different groups, and Egyptian evidence also shows members of some groups in royal service. The Peleset of Ramesses III's inscriptions are generally associated with the Philistines. The surviving texts and early Iron Age material culture help explain population movements in the eastern Mediterranean, while the relationships between individual peoples and biblical names vary in certainty.

## Sources

The label was popularized in modern scholarship by Gaston Maspero. Merneptah's account of his fifth-year war names groups fighting with Libyans, including the Ekwesh, Teresh, Lukka, Sherden and Shekelesh. The year-eight inscription of Ramesses III at [[medinet-habu-sea-peoples|Medinet Habu]] lists Peleset, Tjeker, Shekelesh, Denyen and Weshesh. Reliefs show encounters on land and water as part of the pharaoh's presentation of victory. Sherden also occur earlier in Egyptian service, including in the [[kadesh-battle-reliefs|record of Kadesh]]. The lists therefore document several relationships with Egypt over time. [@anet, 260–263] [@anep, figs. 345–348]

Letters from the last decades of [[ugarit|Ugarit]] refer to enemy ships and attacks. The references are important evidence for danger along the coast, though they do not supply a list identifying the attackers with the groups named in Egypt. [@cos3, 3.45]

## History

The weakening or destruction of many Late Bronze Age centers coincided with disruptions to trade and political systems, local conflict and movements of people. Drought and earthquakes have been proposed for particular regions or episodes. The Egyptian texts describe enemies within this changing political world and portray pharaonic victory in conventional terms. Early Iron Age architecture, cuisine and pottery in parts of the southern coastal plain show links with the Aegean and Cyprus alongside local continuity. The material supports migration and interaction. Specific objects rarely identify the people who used them by name. Identifications such as that of the Denyen with the tribe of Dan remain speculative. [@mazar-archaeology, 300–318] [@neaehl]

## In the Bible

Amos 9:7 and Jer 47:4 associate the [[philistines|Philistines]] with Caphtor, often identified with Crete or the Aegean region. Deut 2:23 depicts people from Caphtor settling near Gaza. Gen 10:14 puts the notice about Philistine descent after the Casluhim, and some scholars propose that it originally referred to the Caphtorim. Ezek 25:16 and Zeph 2:5 associate Cherethites with the Philistine coast. The name is sometimes connected with Crete. The Cherethites and Pelethites in David's service in 2 Sam 8:18 could be foreign troops, though their origins remain uncertain. [@abd, 5:1059–1061]

Josh 17:11 places Dor within Manasseh's allotted towns. The [[report-of-wenamun|*Report of Wenamun*]] portrays it as a town of the Tjeker in its setting at the transition to the Iron Age. The two accounts offer different perspectives on the same coastal town. [@ael, 2:224–230] [@neaehl, 1:357–359]
