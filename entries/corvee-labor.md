## Overview

Corvée is compulsory labor required by a ruler or state. In the ancient Near East it supplied workers for agriculture, transport, and building, among other tasks. A labor obligation could take the form of temporary service by subjects or more lasting servitude imposed on a conquered population. A letter from Late Bronze Age Canaan documents such a levy centuries before the biblical monarchy. In Exodus and Kings, compulsory labor reveals how rulers mobilized workers and what could happen when people regarded the burden as excessive.

## Ancient Near Eastern Evidence

In the fourteenth century BCE, Biridiya, ruler of Megiddo, wrote to the king of Egypt about the cultivation of fields at Shunem. He complained that he alone was supplying laborers and that nearby rulers were failing to contribute. His letter, preserved among the [[amarna-letters|Amarna letters]], refers to the workers by a term related to Hebrew *mas* (“labor levy”). It shows that both the institution and related vocabulary were present in Canaan before the emergence of Israel [@moran-amarna].

Egyptian sources also show authorities assigning work for public or royal projects. Paintings in the [[tomb-of-rekhmire|tomb of Rekhmire]] depict workers making bricks under supervision, including people identified as foreign captives. The paintings provide a comparison for the organization of work in Exod 1:11, where overseers impose labor and the Israelites build storage cities. The workers' legal status and the duration of their obligations could vary from one setting to another [@anep].

## In the Hebrew Bible

Kings assigns a labor levy from all Israel to [[solomon|Solomon]], who sends workers to Lebanon in rotations, each group spending one month there and two months at home (1 Kgs 5:13–18; MT 5:27–32). Another passage assigns a lasting forced levy to populations identified as non-Israelite and says that Solomon made no Israelites bondservants (1 Kgs 9:15–22). The passages present a tension. Some interpreters distinguish temporary service by Israelites from the lasting status assigned to conquered peoples. Others understand the passages as preserving different accounts of who bore the burden of building.

Judges describes people in conquered or partly controlled places being subjected to labor (Judg 1:28–35). Deut 20:11 includes labor and service among the terms imposed on a city that surrenders. The political cost of such demands is prominent in the account of the kingdom's division. The people ask Rehoboam to lighten the hard service of his father. Later they kill Adoram, the official in charge of the levy, when Rehoboam sends him to them (1 Kgs 12:4, 18).

## Interpretive Significance

The [[mesad-hashavyahu-ostracon|Meṣad Ḥashavyahu ostracon]], a late seventh-century BCE petition written on a pottery fragment, describes a harvest worker complaining that an overseer confiscated his garment. This is direct evidence of a worker appealing to an official. His status as a corvée worker is debated because the petition does not specify the legal basis of his service. The appeal invites comparison with protections for a poor person's garment in Exod 22:26–27 (MT 22:25–26) and Deut 24:12–13, although those laws concern pledges [@dobbs-allsopp-hebrew-inscriptions].
