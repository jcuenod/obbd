## Overview

Memphis was a major Egyptian city near the head of the Nile Delta, at and around modern Mit Rahina. It served as a royal and administrative center and was the chief home of the god Ptah. Assyria captured it in 671 BCE, and it remained important under Persian rule. Hebrew prophets name the city in warnings directed against Egypt and in accounts of Judeans living there. Its history helps explain why Memphis could stand for Egyptian authority, offer a refuge to immigrants, and appear as a center of worship in biblical oracles.

## Location and Identification

Memphis lay south of modern Cairo near the division of the Nile into Delta branches. The English name comes through Greek from an Egyptian name conventionally transliterated *Mn-nfr*. Most Hebrew references call it *Nōp*, and Hos 9:6 has the form *Mōp*. The identification rests on the history of these names as well as the position of the ancient city [@abd].

## Archaeological History

The settlement and its extensive cemeteries preserve remains from many periods, although the Nile plain and later occupation complicate recovery of the city's changing layout. Memphis was a center of the cult of Ptah. The [[shabaka-stone|Shabaka Stone]] preserves a theological composition that gives him a central role in creation. Royal building and temple activity made Memphis a center of authority well beyond any single dynasty [@ael].

[[esarhaddon|Esarhaddon]] describes his capture of Memphis in 671 BCE during his campaign against Egypt. The city retained its administrative importance in later periods, including under the Achaemenids. Material and written evidence points to communities with connections outside Egypt, including Phoenicians and Carians. Jeremiah provides literary evidence that Judeans also lived in the city. Such communities belong to different periods and do not all depend on the same kind of evidence [@rinap4] [@tad].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Isa 19:13 holds the leaders of Memphis and other Egyptian centers responsible for leading Egypt astray. Jer 2:16 depicts people of Memphis and Tahpanhes humiliating Judah, using an image of injury to the head. Jer 46:14, 19 names Memphis in an oracle that announces disaster for Egypt. Ezek 30:13, 16 invokes Memphis in a prophecy of judgment, including the destruction of images. The city's prominence as a center of rule and cult helps explain its repeated appearance in such passages.

Jer 44:1 addresses Judeans at Memphis as well as at Migdol, Tahpanhes, and in Pathros. The distribution places Judean communities at more than one point along the Nile and its approaches, in a setting distinct from the better-documented group at [[elephantine|Elephantine]]. Hos 9:6 says that Memphis will bury those who go to Egypt. The city's burial landscape gives an apt association to the wording, while the oracle's primary force is the reversal of a hoped-for refuge.
