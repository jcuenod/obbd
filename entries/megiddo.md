## Overview

Megiddo was a fortified city overlooking the Jezreel Valley and a route across the Carmel range. Its long occupation produced Bronze Age temples and administrative records, Iron Age gates and palaces, and a major water system. Egyptian records describe a battle there, and its Iron Age remains are central to debates about Solomon's reign. Biblical texts place conflicts and royal deaths at Megiddo, and its name is commonly connected with Armageddon in Revelation. The site's strategic position explains why very different texts associate it with military and political power.

## Location and Identification

Ancient Megiddo is identified with the tell at modern Tel Megiddo, near the opening of Wadi Ara into the Jezreel Valley. It controlled access between major coastal and inland routes. Its name occurs in Egyptian records and the [[amarna-letters|Amarna letters]], and its occupation sequence and geographical position support the identification [@neaehl, 5:1943–1950] [@moran-amarna, EA 242–246, 365].

## Archaeological History

Thutmose III's annals describe a siege of Megiddo in the fifteenth century BCE and give an unusually detailed Egyptian account of the campaign. Biridiya of Megiddo later wrote to the Egyptian court, documenting the city's place in the system of local rulers under Egypt. A much later fragment bearing [[shoshenq-i|Shoshenq I's]] name was found in early excavation spoil. The object's original form has been reconsidered, and it lacks a stratified context. It attests an Egyptian royal monument or installation at Megiddo, with limits on how closely it can be connected to a particular layer [@ael, 2:29–35] [@moran-amarna, EA 365] [@neaehl, 5:1943–1950].

The Iron Age city had monumental palaces, successive gates, and pillared buildings. The relation of the six-chambered gate and the palaces to Strata VA–IVB and IVA has raised questions about their sequence and builders. Yigael Yadin associated major buildings with Solomon, while Israel Finkelstein, David Ussishkin, and others have proposed Omride or later settings for some structures. This disagreement belongs to the [[low-chronology-debate|Iron Age chronology debate]]. Pillared buildings of Stratum IVA have been interpreted as stables, stores, or barracks. Recent excavators emphasize the case for stables in the eighth-century city. The site's [[cisterns-and-water-systems|water system]] provided access to water from inside its defenses [@neaehl, 5:1943–1950] [@mazar-archaeology, 381–387].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Josh 12:21 includes Megiddo's king among defeated rulers. Judg 1:27 presents the city as a place where Manasseh failed to remove the inhabitants, and Judg 5:19 places fighting near the waters of Megiddo. 1 Kgs 4:12 places the city in an administrative district, and 1 Kgs 9:15 attributes building there to Solomon. The reference in 1 Kgs 10:26 to chariot cities gives background for the stable interpretation. It does not name the pillared buildings or assign them a date.

2 Kgs 9:27 says that Ahaziah fled to Megiddo and died there. In 2 Kgs 23:29–30, [[josiah|Josiah]] meets Pharaoh Neco there and is killed. Zech 12:11 places mourning for Hadad-rimmon in the plain of Megiddo. Rev 16:16 gives the name Armageddon to a place of gathering within a vision. A derivation from Hebrew *har mĕgiddô*, “mountain of Megiddo,” is widely proposed. Its exact form and the way the vision uses the geographical association remain debated.
