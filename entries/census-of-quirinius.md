## Overview

In 6 CE Rome removed Archelaus from power in Judea and assessed property there under Publius Sulpicius Quirinius, the governor of Syria. The Jewish historian Josephus connects the assessment with resistance led by Judas the Galilean. Acts also remembers Judas and the enrollment. Luke places an enrollment under Quirinius in its account of Jesus' birth, when Joseph and Mary travel to Bethlehem. The documented census took place about a decade after the generally accepted date of Herod the Great's death, creating a chronological problem for the birth account.

## Sources

[[josephus|Josephus]] describes the deposition of Archelaus in *Jewish Antiquities* and connects it with the coming of Quirinius to assess property. He dates the completion of the assessment by counting from Augustus's victory at Actium. An inscription commemorating Quintus Aemilius Secundus also says that he conducted a census of Apamea, a city in Syria, at Quirinius's command. The inscription independently shows Quirinius's involvement in such work, while Josephus supplies the setting and date for the assessment in Judea. [@abd]

## Reconstruction

Archelaus had ruled Judea under Roman authority. His removal brought the territory under more direct Roman administration. In Josephus's account, Judas the Galilean and his supporters treated the assessment as an infringement of liberty. Acts 5:37 likewise mentions Judas drawing followers in the days of an enrollment, though it gives no date and concentrates on the eventual dispersal of his followers.

Roman census measures varied with population, location and purpose. The [[res-gestae|record of Augustus's achievements]] describes censuses of Roman citizens. There is no independent evidence for a simultaneous empire-wide enrollment of the kind commonly understood from Luke. An Egyptian edict issued in 104 CE required those away from home to return for registration. Its language concerns the place where people lived and were registered and provides little support for requiring a journey to a distant ancestral town. [@abd]

## Biblical Accounts

Luke 2:1–5 associates the first enrollment with Quirinius and gives Joseph's Davidic descent as the reason for the journey from Nazareth to [[bethlehem|Bethlehem]]. Luke 1:5 sets the events leading to John's birth in the days of Herod, and Matt 2:1 explicitly places Jesus' birth before [[herod-the-great|Herod's]] death, usually dated to 4 BCE. These settings are hard to reconcile with the Judean census recorded by Josephus. Most critical scholars see a chronological conflation or understand the census notice as serving a narrative purpose in connecting Jesus to David's city. [@abd]

Proposals for an earlier enrollment include identifying Quirinius as the unnamed official in a fragmentary inscription from Tivoli, reading the Greek *prōtē* as “before,” or appealing to Tertullian's attribution of a census to Saturninus. The inscription does not securely identify Quirinius, the proposed translation is contested, and Tertullian's notice comes from a much later source. These proposals have not won broad acceptance.
