## Overview

Gezer was a major city where the southern Levantine coastal plain meets the foothills west of Jerusalem. Its location gave it access to inland routes and traffic along the plain. Written sources attest the city in the Late Bronze Age, when it had its own rulers and Egypt exercised power in Canaan. The site is known for a row of large standing stones, a fortified Iron Age gate, and the inscribed [[gezer-calendar|Gezer Calendar]]. Biblical texts remember it as a Canaanite city, a place rebuilt by Solomon, and, under the name Gazara, a Hasmonean stronghold.

## Location and Identification

Tel Gezer stands above the Aijalon Valley, near routes running from the plain toward the central highlands. Rock-cut inscriptions in the surrounding area mark the boundary of Gezer and helped confirm the ancient city's location. Several also carry Greek writing. Their date and the nature of the property or territory they marked are debated.

R. A. S. Macalister led large-scale excavations between 1902 and 1909. The work recovered many objects and architectural remains, but its recording methods make some finds hard to associate with a particular layer. Later expeditions sponsored by Hebrew Union College and the Tandy Institute investigated the sequence of buildings and destruction deposits with finer stratigraphic control.

## Archaeological History

Gezer was a fortified city during the Middle Bronze Age. A row of ten [[massebot|standing stones]], conventionally called the high place, is among the best-known remains. The stones differ in size and form, and interpretations of the grouping range from cultic commemoration to a monument connected with political agreements. The installation provides physical evidence for the use of standing stones on a large scale.

In the Late Bronze Age, Gezer's rulers corresponded with the Egyptian court in the [[amarna-letters|Amarna letters]]. Gezer also appears on the [[merneptah-stele|Merneptah Stele]], which claims that the pharaoh captured the city. These records reflect different kinds of Egyptian involvement in a strategically placed Canaanite city. Correlating individual texts with excavated horizons requires evidence for the date of each deposit and the event that produced it.

The Iron Age remains include a gate with three chambers on each side of the passage. The gate and related fortifications have often been connected with Solomon's building program, while advocates of a [[low-chronology-debate|lower chronology]] have placed the corresponding building phase in the ninth century BCE. Radiocarbon modeling by the Tandy expedition favors the early tenth century for the beginning of the stratum associated with the fortifications and gate. The date is a finding about a building phase. The identity of the people who commissioned it is a further historical question.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Josh 10:33 describes Horam of Gezer coming to the aid of Lachish against Joshua. Both Josh 16:10 and Judg 1:29 report a continuing Canaanite presence, and Joshua adds that the inhabitants were subjected to forced labor. Josh 21:21 assigns Gezer to the Levites with its pasture lands. In 2 Sam 5:25 it marks the extent of David's pursuit of the Philistines. These references make sense of a well-known city on a corridor between regions.

In 1 Kgs 9:15–17 an unnamed pharaoh captures and burns Gezer and gives it as a wedding gift to his daughter, Solomon's wife. Solomon then rebuilds it. The Egyptian king Siamun is a frequent proposal for the unnamed pharaoh, but direct evidence for the identification is lacking. 1 Macc 13:43–48 recounts Simon's later conquest, cleansing, and strengthening of Gazara. The episode shows that the city's position remained valuable in the Hellenistic period.
