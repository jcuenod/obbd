## Overview

Decorated terracotta stands from Taanach illuminate religious imagery in the southern Levant during the early Iron Age. The best known, found by Paul Lapp in 1968, combines a naked female figure, lions, winged sphinxes, a tree with flanking animals, and a winged sun disk. Its imagery draws on motifs shared across the region. The stand is especially important for discussions of a goddess, the sacred tree, and the representation of divine presence. No inscription names the figures, and both the function of the stand and the relationship among its images remain open to interpretation.

## Description and Provenance

Taanach lies on the southwestern edge of the Jezreel Valley. Ernst Sellin recovered an ornate terracotta stand during his early twentieth-century excavations. Lapp's later excavations recovered a second elaborate example from a cistern deposit. The two major stands differ in their treatment of figures and openings. They attest the repeated use of decorated stands at the site, but recovery from secondary contexts prevents assigning them securely to a particular sanctuary or household. The Lapp stand is now in the Israel Museum. It is generally placed around the transition from Iron I to Iron II, with more specific dates depending on assessments of context and style [@neaehl; @keel-uehlinger-ggig].

## Contents

From bottom to top, the Lapp stand displays a naked female with her hands at the heads of two lions, two winged sphinxes flanking an opening, a stylized tree with animals browsing on either side and lions farther out, and a four-legged animal beneath a winged sun disk. The uppermost quadruped has been called a calf or a horse. Individual motifs belong to a wider Levantine visual repertoire. The staged combination and the empty space between the sphinxes give the object its particular importance.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

The bronze stands for basins in 1 Kgs 7:27–37 bore reliefs of lions, cattle, and cherubim. The biblical stands are different in material and construction, but the description attests the suitability of animal imagery for ritual furniture. Winged creatures arranged on either side of an opening invite comparison with the cherubim associated with Yahweh's throne in 1 Sam 4:4. The parallel concerns composition. It cannot identify the figure or presence associated with the opening at Taanach.

The sacred tree has figured in debates about the *asherah* prohibited in Deut 16:21, and the quadruped beneath the disk has been discussed alongside the horses dedicated to the sun in 2 Kgs 23:11. The latter text concerns a much later setting. Taanach appears in the Song of Deborah and in Solomon's administrative list (Judg 5:19; 1 Kgs 4:12). Neither reference describes the use of the stand.

## Scholarly Issues

Some readings associate the tree and the naked woman with Asherah, and the blank between sphinxes or the disk above the quadruped with Yahweh or a solar deity. Others read the images through regional conventions of divine protection, fertility, and sovereignty. Selective comparison can yield multiple identities for the same tier. The artifact establishes the association of motifs on a ritual object more securely than it establishes the names of its intended recipients.
