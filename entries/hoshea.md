## Overview

Hoshea son of Elah was the last king of the northern kingdom of Israel in the late eighth century BCE. An Assyrian inscription says that Tiglath-pileser III installed him, while Kings tells how he replaced Pekah. He later withheld tribute from Shalmaneser V and sought Egyptian support, according to the Bible. His arrest preceded the reported capture of Samaria. The sources offer complementary views of the pressures on a small kingdom under Assyrian power, although they leave the identity of Hoshea's Egyptian contact and the final stages of Samaria's fall disputed.

## Extrabiblical Sources

An inscription of Tiglath-pileser III names the removal of [[pekah|Pekah]], the installation of Hoshea, and tribute received. The damaged wording leaves some uncertainty about who the inscription says killed Pekah. Tiglath-pileser plainly claims to have made Hoshea king. This is direct evidence of Assyria's role in the transition, and its official viewpoint puts the initiative in the Assyrian ruler's hands [@rinap1].

The Babylonian Chronicle attributes the ravaging of Samaria to [[shalmaneser-v|Shalmaneser V]]. Sargon II's inscriptions claim victory over Samaria and the removal and resettlement of inhabitants. Those sources provide evidence for Assyrian action at the end of the kingdom and under its successor administration. They differ in genre and purpose [@abc] [@glassner-chronicles] [@cos2].

## Biblical Portrayal

In 2 Kgs 15:30, Hoshea plots against Pekah, kills him, and takes his place. The report of a local coup fits a context in which Assyria could recognize or direct a replacement. 2 Kgs 17:1–6 says that Hoshea served Shalmaneser V and paid tribute, then sent messengers to So, king of Egypt, and withheld the regular payment. The king of Assyria imprisons Hoshea. The narrative proceeds to the siege and fall of [[samaria|Samaria]], with Hoshea no longer at liberty to lead the kingdom.

2 Kgs 18:9–11 likewise names Shalmaneser as the king who laid siege, then describes the capture after three years and subsequent [[deportation|deportations]]. Hos 7:11 and 12:1 (MT 12:2) criticize Israel's turns toward Egypt and Assyria. The prophetic texts capture the problem of divided commitments, although they give no detailed account of Hoshea's negotiations.

## Historical Assessment

The name So in 2 Kgs 17:4 has not been securely identified. Osorkon IV, a ruler in the Nile Delta, is one candidate. Some scholars connect the word with Sais in the western Delta and associate the contact with Tefnakht, while others have proposed a direct identification with Tefnakht on other grounds. The political fragmentation of Egypt at the time makes more than one interlocutor possible.

Hoshea's capture is often placed around 724 BCE in a reconstruction of his reign. The dates and sequence of the [[fall-of-samaria|fall of Samaria]] are debated. Shalmaneser V may have taken the city around 722 BCE, and Sargon II may have suppressed a later revolt around 720 BCE or taken credit for his predecessor's conquest. The biblical and Assyrian evidence securely place Hoshea and the end of his kingdom in the setting of Assyrian [[tribute-and-vassalage|vassalage]], revolt, and provincial reorganization.
