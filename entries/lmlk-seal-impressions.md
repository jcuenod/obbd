## Overview

The Hebrew letters *lmlk*, meaning “belonging to the king,” occur on thousands of stamped storage-jar handles from Iron Age Judah. Most of the stamps combine the royal label with an emblem and one of four names. Many jars were in use at Lachish before its destruction in 701 BCE, giving a firm chronological anchor. The stamps illuminate the Judahite monarchy's organization of collection and storage. The contents of individual jars, the duration of the stamping program, and its precise relationship to Hezekiah's preparations remain subjects of research.

## Archaeological Evidence

The handle impressions were made before the clay was fired. They commonly show a four-winged scarab or a two-winged emblem often described as a [[winged-sun-disk|winged disk]]. Some handles bear impressions of private seals associated with the royal jar series. Later stamp programs include rosette impressions, whether or not they served exactly the same administrative functions [@avigad-sass-wsss] [@keel-uehlinger-ggig].

The four names are Hebron, Ziph, Socoh, and *mmšt*. The first three correspond to place names known from biblical Judah. The fourth has been understood as a place name or connected with a term for government or dominion. The marking could refer to estates, collection districts, or points in production and distribution. The impressions attest organization by the monarchy without explaining the administrative system in a single label.

David Ussishkin's work at [[lachish|Lachish]] linked many stamped jars with Level III, destroyed in Sennacherib's campaign of 701 BCE. Oded Lipschits, Omer Sergi, and Ido Koch have argued that some two-winged subtypes continued into the seventh century. Their chronology includes some two-winged stamps in the pre-701 group as well. Context by context, the surviving handles give stronger evidence for the use of particular types than for a single date for the whole system [@neaehl].

## In the Bible

2 Kgs 18:13–16 describes Judah's losses and tribute during [[sennacherib-campaign-701|Sennacherib's campaign]]. The Lachish destruction gives an independent context for stamped storage jars within that event. 2 Chr 32:2–5 attributes defensive preparations to [[hezekiah|Hezekiah]], and 2 Chr 32:27–29 names royal storehouses for grain, wine, and oil. The accounts make royal collection and storage recognizable uses for the jars. The objects do not specify whether they were first produced to meet the emergency or formed part of a longer system.

Josh 15:35, 48, 54–55 names Socoh, Hebron, and Ziph. There is more than one Socoh in the list, so a name alone leaves the identification of the stamping district open. 1 Chr 4:23 describes potters associated with the king's work. That notice offers a possible comparison for organized production, although it does not identify the makers of the stamped vessels.
