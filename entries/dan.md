## Overview

Tel Dan is a settlement mound beside a major source of the Jordan River, at the northern edge of the Hula Valley. Biblical texts call the earlier town Laish and associate Iron Age Dan with a sanctuary of the northern kingdom. Archaeologists have uncovered a well-preserved mudbrick gate from the Middle Bronze Age, later gates and fortifications, and the remains of a sacred precinct used in several periods. Inscriptions from the site illuminate the history of Israel and its neighbors and help confirm the identification of the mound with ancient Dan.

## Location and Identification

The Dan spring and surrounding streams gave the settlement access to abundant water and to routes into the southern Beqaa and toward Damascus. The name Laish probably appears in texts from Mari and in Egyptian lists of places or enemies. These identifications are plausible rather than certain. A much later Greek and Aramaic dedication found in the sacred precinct names the god at Dan. It provides firmer evidence for the identification of the mound. [@neaehl]

## Archaeological History

The Middle Bronze Age town was defended by an earthen rampart and had a gate built of mudbrick with arched passages. The gate was buried in antiquity, which helped preserve it. It is sometimes called Abraham's Gate because Gen 14:14 places Abram's pursuit as far as Dan. That name is a modern association. The narrative itself uses the later name and cannot establish that Abram passed through the excavated gate. A separate complex of stone [[city-gates|gates and fortifications]] belongs to the Iron Age and later use of the site. [@neaehl] [@mazar-archaeology]

The remains in the sacred precinct include a stone platform and evidence for ritual activity, including altar fragments, bronze shovels, and deposits associated with offerings. Avraham Biran linked phases of the precinct with the royal sanctuary described in Kings and attributed some construction to named Israelite rulers. Later studies disagree over the dating of individual structures, and some substantial features previously assigned to the Iron Age have been reassigned to later centuries. The physical remains establish worship at Dan more securely than they identify individual patrons. The fragments of the [[tel-dan-stele|Tel Dan Stele]] were found in reuse near the Iron Age gate. [@neaehl] [@ahituv-echoes]

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Judg 18 connects Dan's name and sanctuary with the arrival of the Danites and their installation of an image formerly kept by Micah. First Samuel uses Dan and Beersheba as northern and southern markers (1 Sam 3:20). In 1 Kgs 12:28–30 a different account connects the sanctuary with Jeroboam and with the calf he placed at Dan, one of a pair with [[bethel|Bethel]]. The excavated precinct provides a setting for such accounts, while the connection of its individual phases with Jeroboam's construction remains disputed.

Dan also appears in the list of places attacked by the Damascene ruler [[ben-hadad|Ben-hadad]] in 1 Kgs 15:20. Amos 8:14 refers to an oath involving the town's god. The Hellenistic dedication supplies later evidence for local worship associated with the place, but the long interval makes continuity in the identity of the god or details of worship difficult to determine.
