## Overview

Solomon is described in Kings as the son of David who built in Jerusalem and other cities and traded with foreign rulers. His reign is conventionally placed in the tenth century BCE. No known contemporary inscription names him. Archaeology documents important changes at some of the cities associated with him, as well as copper production and trade in the surrounding region. The historical question is how far those developments can be related to a single ruler and to the scale of government portrayed in Kings.

## Extrabiblical Sources

Egyptian inscriptions of [[shoshenq-i|Shoshenq I]], usually identified as the biblical Shishak, document a campaign in the Levant around 925 BCE. This gives a chronological point of reference near the biblical division of the kingdom. The conventional dates for Solomon rely on working backward through biblical regnal figures. Shoshenq's records do not give Solomon's name or length of reign [@anet] [@ael].

Excavated gates and other major buildings at [[gezer|Gezer]], [[megiddo|Megiddo]], and [[hazor|Hazor]] became central to the debate over Solomon's building. The buildings have different local sequences. Radiocarbon work at Gezer supports major tenth-century construction in the excavators' chronology, while some proposed dates at the other sites are later. Neither a date nor architectural resemblance by itself names a builder [@neaehl] [@mazar-archaeology].

## Biblical Portrayal

1 Kgs 9:15–19 associates Solomon with construction at Jerusalem and several strategic cities. 1 Kgs 5:1–12 (MT 5:15–26) links the building with timber supplied by [[hiram-of-tyre|Hiram of Tyre]]. Later traditions about Tyrian kings preserved by Josephus provide a comparison, while no contemporary Tyrian document establishes the arrangement. The fleet to Ophir in 1 Kgs 9:26–28 and the gifts of the queen of [[sheba|Sheba]] in 1 Kgs 10:1–13 likewise fit wider networks of travel and exchange. Their reported scale and individual events cannot be verified independently.

1 Kgs 10:28–29 depicts trade in horses and chariots involving Egypt and Kue, often associated with Cilicia. 1 Kgs 11:14–25 presents opposition in Edom and at Damascus, giving a more complex political picture than the description of rule from the Euphrates to Egypt in 1 Kgs 4:21 (MT 5:1). 1 Kgs 11:40 already names Shishak as the ruler to whom Jeroboam flees.

## Historical Assessment

Debates over the [[low-chronology-debate|Iron Age chronology]] concern individual construction levels, the onset of monumental building, and the reach of political authority. Copper production at Timna and [[faynan|Faynan]] expanded in the early Iron Age and was substantial around the tenth century. The evidence for industrial activity does not identify Jerusalem as its governing authority, and scholars differ over the political organization of local producers. These regional developments provide context for Solomon's portrayal, while his precise historical role and the extent of his control remain debated.
