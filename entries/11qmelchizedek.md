## Overview

11QMelchizedek is a fragmentary Hebrew work from Qumran Cave 11. It interprets biblical laws about release and prophetic language about good news and judgment as descriptions of deliverance at the end of the age. Melchizedek appears in a heavenly role at the center of that deliverance. The scroll shows how some Second Temple Jews interpreted Jubilee and Isaiah as promises of future restoration. Its treatment of Melchizedek also provides a comparison for the figure's different role in Hebrews.

## Description and Provenance

The fragments are catalogued as 11Q13 among the [[dead-sea-scrolls]]. Most continuous evidence comes from the reconstructed second column. The surviving pieces preserve only part of the composition, so the placement of fragments and the restoration of damaged lines affect how several passages are read [@djd23, 11Q13].

## Contents

The second column brings together the return to ancestral property in Lev 25:8–13 and the release of debts in Deut 15:1–2. It applies their language to a final release for the people assigned to Melchizedek. The column then brings in [[divine-council|divine assembly]] language from Psalm 82 in connection with judgment. The language of proclaiming liberty and good news draws on Isaiah 61 and 52. The author makes these passages explain one another [@cekiera-melchizedek, 411–439].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

The scroll and Luke 4:16–21 both read Isa 61:1–2 as language of fulfillment and deliverance. The scroll draws that language into a timetable of judgment and release. Luke uses it in Jesus' announcement at Nazareth. The comparison shows the range of ways in which Isaiah was read in this period.

Heb 7:1–17 develops an argument about Jesus' priesthood through the Melchizedek of Genesis 14 and Psalm 110. The scroll's Melchizedek is principally an agent of deliverance and judgment in the surviving column. Exalting the same figure makes the texts comparable, while their choice of scriptural passages and the work they assign him differ.

## Scholarly Issues

Melchizedek is commonly understood here as a heavenly figure, but his precise status and relation to other heavenly agents remain debated. Some interpretations compare him with Michael. The surviving text leaves that identification open. The scroll is evidence for the interpretation of scripture in the Second Temple period. It does not independently attest the person in Genesis 14 or establish a connection between its authors and the writers of Luke or Hebrews.
