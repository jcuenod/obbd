## Overview

Shiloh is an ancient site in the central hill country remembered in Joshua, Judges, and Samuel as a place of Israelite worship and the ark. The mound at Khirbet Seilun preserves Bronze Age remains and an Iron Age I settlement with storage jars and buildings destroyed by fire. The site’s location fits the Bible’s directions. The excavated remains show that Shiloh was an important place in the period associated with the traditions, while the sanctuary’s location and the cause of the destruction remain uncertain.

## Location and Identification

Khirbet Seilun lies north of Bethel in the hill country of the West Bank. The preservation of the name and the textual directions in Judg 21:19 support the identification. Routes between Bethel, Shechem, and settlements along the ridge passed through the wider area. The site’s importance need not imply that it controlled a large territory or occupied the same role throughout its history.

## Archaeological History

Danish excavations beginning in the 1920s and subsequent work directed by Israel Finkelstein documented occupation in more than one period. Bronze Age finds include substantial remains followed by evidence of activity that is unevenly distributed on the mound. Iron Age I buildings supported by rows of pillars contained large storage jars, some with a thickened collar below the rim. The buildings and jars document organized storage. Whether the goods were connected with a sanctuary or another form of administration remains a matter of interpretation.

Parts of the Iron Age I settlement ended in a fire, usually placed in the eleventh century BCE. The destruction is archaeologically visible. The identity of those responsible is not. Proposals by later excavators to identify the tabernacle site or certain stones as pieces of horned altars have generated discussion without securing broad acceptance. No excavated structure can presently be identified with confidence as the sanctuary of the biblical narratives.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Josh 18:1 places the [[tabernacle|tent of meeting]] at Shiloh. Judg 18:31 treats worship there as a chronological point of reference, and 21:19–21 situates an annual festival in relation to the town and its surroundings. First Samuel 1:3, 9 describes visits and worship at Shiloh, and 4:3–11 has the Israelites bring the [[ark-of-the-covenant|ark]] from Shiloh to the battlefield, where it is lost. The story does not report an attack on Shiloh itself.

Ps 78:60 recalls the abandonment of the sanctuary, and Jer 7:12–14 and 26:6, 9 use Shiloh to warn Jerusalem of comparable loss. Some scholars connect those memories and the Iron Age destruction with the Philistine victory in Samuel. The archaeological fire can illuminate the comparison, but it cannot establish that the same battle led to the settlement’s fall. First Kings 14:2 locates the prophet Ahijah at Shiloh in a later setting. It shows that the place remained significant in biblical memory after the episodes concerning the ark.
