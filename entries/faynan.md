## Overview

Faynan is a copper-producing district on the eastern side of the Arabah valley in southern Jordan. Mines, furnaces, and extensive heaps of industrial waste document production over many centuries. At Khirbat en-Naḥas, radiocarbon dating places major activity in the early Iron Age, including the tenth century BCE. These finds have changed accounts of when large-scale production and political organization developed in the region later known as Edom. A proposed identification with biblical Punon provides a possible geographic link to the wilderness itinerary, while connections with particular rulers or campaigns remain debated.

## Location and Identification

Faynan lies at the foot of the mountains overlooking the Arabah, where deposits of copper ore could be reached from the surrounding valleys. Khirbat en-Naḥas, whose modern name means “ruin of copper,” is one of several sites in the district. The region is generally associated with Phaino, a place known in Roman and early Christian accounts for its mines. The similar name Punon in the biblical itinerary has also been associated with Faynan, although the route is less securely fixed.

## Archaeological History

Excavations led by Thomas E. Levy, Mohammad Najjar, and their colleagues exposed buildings, a fortress, and deposits of slag, the waste produced when copper is extracted from ore. Radiocarbon measurements on organic material from excavated layers place large-scale copper production between the eleventh and ninth centuries BCE. Scholars continue to debate the date of particular buildings and phases. The evidence nevertheless shows that extensive production took place long before the best-attested later Edomite centers reached their prominence.

The scale of the industry implies coordination of labor, fuel, resources, and transport. Some archaeologists understand it as the work of an early Edomite polity, possibly with a substantial mobile population. Other scholars propose a less centralized organization or question the connection between these early producers and the later kingdom of Edom. The sites attest well-organized [[copper-production|copper production]], while the political name and boundaries of those who organized it are uncertain.

Egyptian objects found in the district include amulets and a scarab bearing a name of [[shoshenq-i|Shoshenq I]], a ruler of the early Twenty-second Dynasty. Some researchers connect the finds and changes in production with his campaign in the southern Levant. Small portable objects can move through exchange as well as accompany an army. The evidence does not establish that Egyptian forces reached the mines.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Num 33:42–43 places Punon between Zalmonah and Oboth on Israel's wilderness itinerary. An identification with Faynan would supply a regional landmark, while the verse itself says nothing about copper production. The archaeological evidence also provides context for discussions of early [[edomites|Edom]], including the list of its kings in Gen 36:31–39. The site cannot identify individuals in the list or establish the extent of their rule.

2 Sam 8:13–14 and 1 Kgs 11:14–16 depict conflict and Israelite intervention in Edom. The dates of Iron Age copper production overlap with dates often proposed for the reign of David. Some have suggested an association with Israelite or Judahite power, but that interpretation is disputed. The excavations have supplied no inscription identifying David, Solomon, or Joab as controlling the mines.
