## Overview

Dibon was a town in Moab, east of the Dead Sea and north of the Arnon River, at modern Dhiban in Jordan. It is best known as the findspot of the victory inscription of Mesha, a ninth-century BCE king of Moab who identifies himself as a Dibonite. Excavations have found Iron Age buildings and waterworks amid remains from several later periods. The site helps explain Dibon's prominence both in accounts of conflicts east of the Jordan and in biblical prophecies concerning Moab.

## Location and Identification

The mound beside modern Dhiban lies on the Moabite plateau north of Wadi al-Mujib, the biblical Arnon. Its name, geography, and the discovery of the [[mesha-stele|Mesha Stele]] support the identification. The Moabite town is distinct from the Dibon in a list of Judahite settlements in Neh 11:25.

## Archaeological History

The Mesha Stele came to scholarly notice at Dhiban in 1868. Its opening calls Mesha a Dibonite and a king of Moab. The inscription also describes works at a place called Qarhoh, including walls and water storage. Many scholars identify Qarhoh with an elevated or fortified part of Dibon. Its precise location on the mound has not been established [@kai, no. 181] [@cos2, 2.23].

American excavations in the 1950s examined several parts of the site. Later work by the Dhiban Excavation and Development Project clarified some of the Iron Age remains and also investigated much later occupation. Evidence includes a substantial building and features connected with water management. The portions exposed are valuable for the history of the town, but they do not furnish a full plan of Mesha's center or prove that individual remains are works he claimed in the inscription.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Num 21:30 includes Dibon in a poem about the taking of territory east of the Jordan. Num 32:3, 34 includes it among towns requested and built by Gad, while Josh 13:9, 17 places Dibon in a description of Reuben's allotment. These references show the town's importance to different traditions about settlement. The passages do not yield a single consistent account of who controlled it. Num 33:45–46 also gives the compound name Dibon-gad in a list of stopping places. These notices use the town name to organize memories of travel, building, and allotment in different ways.

2 Kgs 3:4–5 describes Mesha's payment to Israel and his later revolt. The stele provides his own account of [[moabites|Moab's]] struggle with Israel and gives Dibon a role in his royal identity. Its description of royal building also helps explain why the town could symbolize Moabite power. Isa 15:2 depicts Dibon in mourning, and Jer 48:18, 22 addresses the city within a wider judgment oracle. The geographic and political setting helps readers understand the force of the prophetic address.
