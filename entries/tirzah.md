## Overview

Tirzah was an early royal center of the northern kingdom of Israel. The books of Kings locate several rulers there before Omri established a capital at Samaria. The city is generally identified with Tell el-Farʿah North, northeast of Nablus. Excavations at the mound have uncovered settlements from several periods, including Iron Age houses and an incomplete monumental building. Archaeologists have compared the building with the change of capital recounted in Kings, while its precise date and relationship to Omri remain debated.

## Location and Identification

Tell el-Farʿah North lies in the hills northeast of [[shechem|Shechem]], near springs and overlooking a route toward the Jordan Valley. This position and the Iron Age remains fit the broad location and importance suggested by the biblical notices on Tirzah. William F. Albright proposed the identification, which is now widely accepted. The site is distinct from the mound of the same Arabic name in the southern coastal region.

## Archaeological History

Roland de Vaux excavated the site between 1946 and 1960. The recovered remains include major Chalcolithic and Early Bronze Age phases, as well as several phases of Iron Age settlement. Some Iron Age houses follow the [[four-room-house|pillared house plan]] known at sites in Israel and Judah. Differences among houses have been interpreted as evidence of increasing social stratification in later Iron Age levels. Plans and objects help show such differences, although house size and form can reflect a range of factors.

An unfinished monumental building has been connected with the move to Samaria described in Kings. De Vaux proposed that a large building project was abandoned when Omri transferred the capital. Reassessment of the Iron Age layers has changed how several phases are dated. The difference in chronological schemes matters for which buildings could have stood during the time of particular kings. See [[low-chronology-debate|The Low Chronology Debate]].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Josh 12:24 lists a king of Tirzah among the defeated kings of Canaan. In Num 26:33, Tirzah is a daughter of Zelophehad. Similar names can have associations with places or groups, but that verse gives the name as a personal name. In 1 Kgs 14:17 Jeroboam's wife goes to Tirzah, and 1 Kgs 15:21, 33 makes it a center for Baasha's reign. Tirzah provides a fixed setting in these otherwise brief royal notices.

In 1 Kgs 16:8–23 Zimri seizes power and then dies after setting fire to the royal house. [[omri|Omri]] rules for six years at Tirzah and then acquires the hill of [[samaria|Samaria]] (1 Kgs 16:24). The unfinished building at Tell el-Farʿah can be compared with this period of change. An excavated building or destruction horizon still requires independent evidence to establish a connection with a named person.

2 Kgs 15:14–16 says that Menahem comes from Tirzah to Samaria, showing Tirzah's continuing importance after it ceased to be the capital. Song 6:4 compares the beloved to Tirzah and Jerusalem. The two cities appear there as images of attractiveness and grandeur.
