## Overview

A Greek inscription from Delphi records the liberation of Antigona and her daughters Theodora and Dorothea in the mid-second century BCE. Antigona is identified as Jewish. The transaction took the legal form of a sale to Pythian Apollo for seven silver minas, with explicit guarantees of the women's freedom. The inscription illustrates one way that language of purchase, freedom, and divine ownership could work together. That combination supplies a useful comparison for Paul's use of purchase and freedom in 1 Corinthians.

## Description and Provenance

The inscription belongs to the group of Greek records of manumission, or release from slavery, preserved at the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi. The edition identifies it as SGDI II 1722 and assigns it to the middle decades of the second century BCE. Its calendar heading gives the local magistrate and month [@delphi-1722, lines 1–16].

## Contents

Ateisidas sells the three women to Apollo for seven silver minas, a unit of monetary reckoning. The text records receipt of the price and names Eudokos of Delphi as guarantor. It declares the women free for life and provides remedies if someone tries to enslave them again. This instrument imposes no continuing service to the former owner. Some other Delphic contracts did impose such service, so that feature must be checked in each text.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

In 1 Cor 6:19–20 Paul invokes purchase as the basis for belonging to God. In 1 Cor 7:21–23 he juxtaposes the slave who is the Lord's freedperson and the free person who is Christ's slave. The Delphic transaction shows how purchase by a god and protection of freedom could appear together in Greek legal language. Paul's argument applies such language to the believer's standing and conduct, without giving a manumission contract or citing the Delphic procedure.

In Phlm 15–16 Paul asks that Onesimus be received as a beloved brother. The inscription shows what explicit legal provision for freedom can look like. Philemon leaves the legal outcome unstated.
