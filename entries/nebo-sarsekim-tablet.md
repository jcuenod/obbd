## Overview

British Museum tablet BM 114789 is a Babylonian cuneiform record of a delivery of gold to the temple of Marduk in Babylon. Dated in the tenth regnal year of Nebuchadnezzar II, it names a senior official, Nabû-šarrūssu-ukīn. The combination of name and title closely resembles that of Nebo-Sarsekim in Jer 39:3. This is a strong comparison for the identification of an official in the biblical account of Jerusalem's fall. The tablet itself concerns a temple transaction several years before the fall.

## Description and Provenance

This small clay tablet belongs to the administrative records associated with Sippar. It is kept in the British Museum as BM 114789, registration 1920,1213.81. The text is in Akkadian cuneiform and gives the month and day in Nebuchadnezzar's tenth year [@bm-nebo-sarsekim] [@jursa-nebo-sarsekim].

## Contents

The tablet records that gold belonging to Nabû-šarrūssu-ukīn was delivered to Esagila, the temple of Marduk in Babylon. It identifies the official by the title *rab ša-rēši* and names the intermediary and witnesses. The transaction illustrates how even a short administrative record can preserve a personal name in conjunction with an official designation.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Jer 39:3 lists Babylonian officials at Jerusalem. Michael Jursa proposed that the name and title there correspond to the pair on the tablet. The chronological setting and the association of both men with [[nebuchadnezzar-ii|Nebuchadnezzar II]] support the proposal. The Hebrew verse's division and reading of names require judgment, and the tablet does not record the official's presence at the siege.

Dan 1:3 describes another senior official in Nebuchadnezzar's service who is designated by a comparable Hebrew title. The tablet gives context for the Babylonian office. It supplies no evidence that Daniel's official was the same man. The literal interpretation of titles traditionally rendered “chief eunuch” and the physical status of titleholders should be considered separately. See [[court-officials]] and [[eunuchs]].
