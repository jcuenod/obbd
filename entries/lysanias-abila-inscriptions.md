## Overview

Greek dedicatory inscriptions from Abila in the Anti-Lebanon name Nymphaeus, a freedman of Lysanias the tetrarch. The fuller text records a public benefaction and associates it with the welfare of the Augusti and their household. Luke 3:1 also gives the name Lysanias and the title tetrarch when locating the beginning of John's activity in the reign of Tiberius. The inscriptions securely attest the name and title in connection with Abila. Whether they name the same man as Luke remains a historical question.

## Manuscripts and Date

The texts survive as stone inscriptions. Raphaël Savignac published the fuller version together with a comparison of the damaged parallel. The conventional early imperial dating of the dedication depends on interpreting the plural *Augusti* as referring to Tiberius and Julia Augusta, also known as Livia. Even on that dating, the inscription dates the freedman's public act. A manumitted person could continue to identify himself by his former master after the master's death [@savignac-abila, inscription A].

## Contents

Nymphaeus names his father, identifies himself as a freedman, and records work undertaken at his own expense. The benefaction included a road, a sanctuary, and plantings. The dedication names Kronos and the fatherland, while the welfare of the Augusti and their house supplies its occasion. The combination illustrates how a local donor could display piety and acknowledge Roman authority in the same inscription. See [[patronage-and-benefaction]] and [[imperial-cult]].

## Significance for Biblical Studies

Luke 3:1 assigns Lysanias to Abilene alongside other regional rulers. The place, name, and title give the comparison its force. Josephus also knows an earlier ruler named Lysanias, executed in the late first century BCE (*Ant.* 15.92). The inscription's relationship to that ruler and to Luke's tetrarch cannot be resolved by the titles alone. It gives no term of office for the man it names. The identification with Luke's ruler should therefore be distinguished from the secure reading of the inscription.
