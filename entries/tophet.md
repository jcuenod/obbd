## Overview

Topheth is the name of a place in the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem where biblical writers condemn the burning of children. Its exact location has not been established by excavation. Archaeologists also use the related term *tophet* for Phoenician and Punic precincts containing urns with cremated remains, especially at Carthage. The use of one name for these different settings reflects a modern comparison. Jeremiah's association of the valley with judgment also forms part of the background to later Jewish and Christian images of punishment.

## Location and Identification

The Valley of Hinnom lies along the southern side of ancient Jerusalem, and part of it curves up the west side. Josh 15:8 names the valley in describing the boundary of Judah. Kings and Jeremiah place Topheth in or in relation to the valley, but archaeological evidence has yet to identify the particular site. The Hebrew name *tōpet* is often understood as a term related to a hearth or fireplace, with vowels that evoke *bōšet*, “shame.” The origin and vocalization of the name remain debated.

## Archaeological History

At Carthage and other Phoenician or Punic sites, including Motya, Tharros, and Sulcis, excavations have found precincts containing urns with cremated remains of infants, children, or animals. Some urns are associated with stelae bearing dedications. Scholars borrowed the biblical term *tophet* for such sites, which belong to their own regional and historical settings. Their conventional name indicates the comparative history of scholarship.

One interpretation understands the precincts as sites of sacrifice. Another allows that some or many infants may have died of other causes and been buried apart from adults. Researchers weigh the inscriptions, ancient literary reports, and estimates of age at death. Cremation affects the surviving bones and teeth, making age estimates methodologically important and difficult. The Punic evidence is a comparison for the Jerusalem texts and leaves the location and physical arrangement of the Judahite site unknown.

## Significance for Biblical Studies

2 Kgs 23:10 attributes the defilement of Topheth to [[josiah|Josiah]] and describes it as a measure against making children pass through fire to [[molek|Molek]]. Jer 7:31–32 condemns high places where children are burned and says the valley will be called the Valley of Slaughter. In Jer 19:6, 11–14 the place of condemned worship becomes the image of the fate of the city and its houses. Isa 30:33 uses a related Hebrew term in a scene of fire prepared for the king. The exact relationship of the term to the Jerusalem site is uncertain.

In 1 Enoch 27 an accursed valley becomes a place of judgment, often understood in connection with the geography around Jerusalem. The Greek name Gehenna reflects the name of the Valley of Hinnom. In Mark 9:43–48, Gehenna is an image of judgment and unquenchable fire. These readings develop associations already visible in Jeremiah, while shifting from the fate of a specific valley and city to a picture of divine judgment.
